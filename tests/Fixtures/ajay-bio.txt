How he teaches

His approach is clarity before complexity. Before a student practises a single problem, they need to understand why a concept works: where a formula comes from, what a graph is really saying, and how an idea in mechanics connects to one in calculus. Once the idea is clear, marks follow.

Every student gets a plan built around their school calendar, their weak areas and their target, whether that is a strong board result, a 7 in IB Maths or Physics, an A* in the IGCSE, or a good JEE rank. A typical cycle looks like this:

• A short diagnostic in the first class to find the real gaps, not just the current chapter
• Concept sessions that build each topic from first principles
• Graded practice, from textbook questions up to board, IB, IGCSE or JEE level
• Weekly tests with detailed feedback and a projection of the marks the student is on track for
• Revision cycles and full-length papers well before school exams and boards
• Regular updates to parents on progress, attendance and what comes next

For board students

For CBSE, ICSE and ISC students in Classes 11 and 12, the focus is complete syllabus coverage with depth: derivations, numericals, graphs and the answer-writing technique that examiners reward, with previous years' papers solved topic by topic. Class 9 and 10 students get the strong foundations in algebra, geometry, mechanics and electricity that the senior classes depend on.

For IB and IGCSE students

IB students get help with both papers and the internal assessment: choosing a workable research question, structuring the exploration or investigation, and meeting the assessment criteria. IGCSE students learn to write precise, mark-scheme answers and practise with past papers under timed conditions. Students switching between an Indian board and the IB or IGCSE get help bridging the gap quickly.

For JEE and NEET aspirants

Entrance preparation runs alongside school studies. Students build speed with graded question sets and take timed tests with error analysis, so the same mistake does not cost marks twice. NEET students get focused Physics preparation built on NCERT concepts and numericals.
