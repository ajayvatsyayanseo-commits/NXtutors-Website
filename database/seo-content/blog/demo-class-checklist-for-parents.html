A demo class is the single best chance you get to judge a tutor before you commit money and months of your child's time. One well-used hour tells you far more than any profile or review.

This checklist shows you what to share and ask before the demo, what to watch during it, what to settle afterwards, the red flags that should make you pause, and when it is time to switch. At the end there is a short checklist you can copy onto your phone or print for the day.

Before the demo: prepare in 15 minutes

What to share with the tutor

Send these details in advance so the tutor can prepare:

  • Class, board and subject – for example, Class 10 ICSE Physics, or IB Maths Analysis and Approaches.
  • The goal – passing comfortably, moving from 70% to 85%, board exam preparation, or JEE/NEET alongside school.
  • Current level – recent test marks and, if you can, a photo of a recent test paper with mistakes.
  • Two or three weak topics – ask your child which chapters they dread.
  • Upcoming dates – unit tests, pre-boards or entrance exams in the next few months.
  • Practical details – preferred days and times, and whether classes will be at home or online.

On NXTutors, when you ask for a tutor, you tell us the subject, class, board and area, and we shortlist two or three matched tutors. The first class is a free demo, so you can use it to test the fit properly.

Questions to ask the tutor

Good tutors answer these easily and specifically.

  1. How many students have you taught in this class and board recently?
  2. How would you plan the next two months for my child?
  3. How do you set and check homework?
  4. How will you tell me about progress, and how often?
  5. What happens if a class is missed, by you or by us?
  6. For exam years: how do you use past papers and timed practice?

During the demo: what to watch

If you can, sit in for part of the class, or at least stay within earshot. For online demos, sit beside your child for the first ten minutes. Watch for these six things.

1. Clarity

Can the tutor explain an idea in simple words, and then again in a different way if your child does not follow?

2. Checking understanding

Strong tutors do not just ask "understood?" – children almost always say yes. They ask your child to solve a similar question, explain a step back, or spot an error. Listen for these checks.

3. Pace

The pace should fit your child, not the tutor's plan for the hour. If your child is lost after ten minutes, or bored because the material is too easy, the tutor should notice and adjust.

4. Engagement

Who is doing most of the talking? In a good class, your child is writing, answering and asking questions for a large part of the time.

5. How mistakes are handled

Watch what happens when your child gets something wrong. The best response is calm and curious: "Let us see where this changed." Impatience, sarcasm or simply correcting the answer without explaining are warning signs.

6. Homework and a plan

By the end, the tutor should be able to say what they noticed, what to work on first and what to practise before the next class. A small, specific piece of homework is a good sign.

After the demo: settle the practical details

Ask your child the right questions

"Did you like the tutor?" invites a one-word answer. Try these instead:

  • What is one thing you understood today that you did not before?
  • Did you feel comfortable asking a question?
  • Was it too fast, too slow or about right?
  • Would you be happy to have this class twice a week?

If your child cannot name one thing they learnt, take that seriously, even if they "liked" the tutor.

Agree the plan, fees and schedule

  • Follow-up plan – ask for a short written plan for the first month: topics, number of classes and how progress will be checked.
  • Fees – as a guide, home tuition on NXTutors is roughly ₹800–₹2,500 per hour, depending on class, subject and experience; senior classes and international boards can cost more. On NXTutors, payment is made in advance, and you get a refund if you cancel within 24 hours of booking.
  • Schedule – fix days, times and class length, and agree what happens around school exams and holidays.
  • A review point – agree to review after the first month. Four weeks is usually enough to see whether homework is being done, whether your child is more confident and whether test marks are moving.

Red flags

  • The tutor did not ask about your child's board, syllabus or recent marks.
  • They spent the demo talking about their results rather than teaching.
  • They could not explain the same idea in a second way.
  • They were on the phone, late, or ended early without reason.
  • They promised specific marks or ranks. No honest tutor can guarantee a result.
  • They pushed for a large advance payment before any plan was agreed.
  • Your child seemed anxious, embarrassed or unwilling to ask questions.

One red flag is worth a conversation. Two or more usually mean you should try another tutor.

The demo class checklist

Copy this list into your phone notes or print it before the class.

Before

  • Class, board, subject and goal shared with the tutor
  • Recent test paper or marks shared
  • Two or three weak topics listed
  • Exam dates and preferred timings shared
  • My questions for the tutor written down

During

  • Explanations were clear and simple
  • Tutor checked understanding with a question, not just "understood?"
  • Pace suited my child
  • My child did plenty of the talking and writing
  • Mistakes were handled calmly and explained
  • Tutor ended with a plan and small homework

After

  • My child can name one thing they learnt
  • My child felt comfortable asking questions
  • First-month plan agreed
  • Fees, payment and cancellation terms clear
  • Days, times and class length fixed
  • Review date set for the end of month one

When to switch tutors

Most children need a few classes to settle, but do not wait a whole term if things are clearly not working. Consider switching if, after about a month:

  • your child still dreads the class or avoids it;
  • there is no plan, or the plan is not being followed;
  • homework is rarely set or checked;
  • you hear nothing about progress unless you ask;
  • test marks and confidence show no movement at all.

Frequently asked questions

Is the demo class really free on NXTutors?

Yes. The first class is a free demo. If the fit is not right, switching tutor is also free.

Should my child be alone with the tutor during the demo?

For the demo, it is sensible for a parent to be present or nearby, at least for part of the class. It helps you judge the teaching and helps your child feel at ease with someone new.

How long should a demo class be?

Long enough for the tutor to teach one idea, check understanding and set a small task. For most school subjects, that means a class of normal length rather than a 15-minute chat.

Can we try more than one tutor?

NXTutors shortlists two or three matched tutors, so you can compare them before choosing. If the first tutor is not the right fit, switching is free. Ask the team how further demo classes work for your booking, and use the same checklist each time so your comparison is fair.

How soon will we see results?

Confidence and homework habits often change within a few weeks. Marks usually take longer, because they depend on the next test and how much ground there is to cover. Agree a review point with the tutor after the first month.
