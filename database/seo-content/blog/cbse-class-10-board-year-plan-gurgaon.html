Class 10 is the first year your child sits a CBSE board exam, and the year goes faster than most families expect. The session starts in April, school tests come quickly, pre-boards arrive in winter, and by then there is little time left to fix gaps. Many students in Gurgaon (Gurugram) also juggle coaching, tuition, school events and long drives across the city, so a plan made in April saves a lot of stress in January.

This guide sets out what CBSE has confirmed for students sitting the 2027 board exam, a month-by-month plan from April to March that matches how most Gurgaon schools run the year, subject-by-subject priorities, how to use sample papers and past papers, how to fit tuition around school and travel, what parents can usefully do, and when a tutor actually helps.

What CBSE has confirmed for the 2027 exam

Rules have changed in the last two years, so it is worth separating what is official from what is doing the rounds on social media. These points come from CBSE's own circulars and the 2026-27 curriculum on cbseacademic.nic.in.

  • 80 + 20 marking. In the main subjects, the board paper is out of 80 and internal assessment by the school is out of 20. CBSE's curriculum sets 33% as the minimum to pass a subject and requires every student to complete both the board exam and the internal assessment.
  • Internal assessment is a year-long job. In maths, the 20 marks are split into pen-paper tests and multiple assessment (5 + 5), a portfolio (5) and lab practical work (5). Science and social science follow a similar pattern of periodic tests, multiple assessment, portfolio and subject enrichment or practical work. These marks are earned from April onwards, not in the final month.
  • Competency-based questions. CBSE's curriculum for 2026-27 states that about 50% of board questions are competency-focused: case-based, source-based, data interpretation and application questions. The rest are MCQs and short and long answers.
  • Maths comes in two levels. Mathematics Standard (041) and Mathematics Basic (241) are both 3-hour papers out of 80, with the same 20-mark internal assessment. The Basic paper puts more weight on remembering and understanding; the Standard paper has more application and analysis.
  • Two board exams for Class 10. CBSE introduced two board examinations for Class 10 from 2026. In 2026, the main exam was compulsory for all students, and passed, eligible students could take an optional second exam to improve their performance in up to three subjects from science, maths, social science and languages; the better of the two performances counts. CBSE's 2026-27 registration circulars refer to the 2027 "main" examination, but the dates for 2027 have not been announced yet. Check CBSE's circulars on cbse.gov.in for the 2027 schedule and any changes to the rules.

One more practical point: CBSE has started the List of Candidates (LOC) process for the 2027 board exams. This is where the school records each student's subjects, including Maths Standard or Basic. Check with the school that your child's details and subjects are correct.

A month-by-month plan for the board year

Most CBSE schools in Gurgaon start the session in April, hold a half-yearly or first-term exam around September, and run one or more pre-boards between December and January. Your school's calendar may differ by a few weeks, so pencil in the real dates as soon as the school shares them.

If your child is aiming for the optional second exam, plan the months after the main exam only once CBSE publishes the 2027 schedule and eligibility. Do not build the whole year around a second attempt; the main exam is compulsory, and internal assessment marks are earned during the year.

Subject-by-subject priorities

Mathematics (Standard or Basic)

Maths rewards steady weekly practice more than any other subject. Algebra carries the most marks in the course structure, followed by geometry and trigonometry, so these deserve regular practice right through the year. Write full steps, because board marking gives credit for method. Choose Standard or Basic with the school's advice, based on your child's comfort with problem-solving and their plans for Class 11; if there is any chance they will take mathematics later, check the current rules with the school before the LOC is final. For a chapter-wise approach, see our CBSE Class 10 maths preparation guide.

Science

Science is really three subjects in one paper: physics, chemistry and biology. The theory paper is set with about half the marks on knowledge and understanding, and the rest on application and higher-order skills, so learning definitions is not enough. Practise labelled diagrams, balanced chemical equations and numericals in electricity and light. Keep the practical file complete, since it feeds internal marks. Our CBSE Class 10 science notes give a chapter-by-chapter summary.

Social science

History, geography, political science and economics carry a lot of reading, and students often leave them too late. A little every week works far better than cramming in February. Practise source-based and case-based questions, and do map work regularly rather than in the last week. The project on consumer rights, social issues or sustainable development counts towards internal assessment, so finish it on time.

English

English papers test reading, writing formats and literature. Read the prescribed texts properly once early in the year, then practise writing tasks against the word limits. The speaking and listening activity is part of internal assessment, so take it seriously.

Second language

Hindi, Sanskrit or a foreign language is often the subject where marks slip because it gets the least time. Keep a fixed weekly slot for it, practise grammar and writing tasks, and solve at least a few sample papers before the pre-boards.

Sample papers and past papers: how to use them

CBSE releases sample question papers and marking schemes in advance on cbseacademic.nic.in. These are the most reliable guide to the current paper pattern, so start with them before any guidebook.

  • Start chapter-wise, then go full-length. From July, pick previous-year questions for each finished chapter. From November, move to full papers.
  • Always use a timer. Three hours at a desk, no phone, no breaks. Many students know the content but run out of time.
  • Mark with the marking scheme. Check where the step marks go. This teaches your child what examiners actually reward.
  • Keep a mistakes notebook. Every wrong answer goes in with the correct method. Revising this notebook in the last month is worth more than another new paper.
  • Remember the pattern shift. Older past papers had fewer case-based questions. Use them for practice, but give extra time to competency-style questions from the current sample papers.

Managing coaching, tuition and travel

Many Class 10 students in Gurgaon add coaching for a future entrance exam, or tuition in one or two subjects. That can work, but a full timetable with evening traffic on top can leave no time for self-study, which is where marks are really made.

  • Count the hours honestly. Add up school, travel, coaching, tuition and homework for a normal week. If self-study is under an hour a day, something needs to go.
  • Cut travel where you can. A cross-city drive at school-run or office hours can take as long as the class itself. Home tuition, or online classes for older, focused students, can give that time back.
  • Put tuition where it is needed. One or two subjects with regular tutor time is usually more useful than tuition in every subject.
  • Protect sleep. Late-night study after a long day rarely sticks. A regular sleep time matters most in the pre-board and board months.

In gated societies, sort out visitor-entry routines for a home tutor at the start, so the first ten minutes of each class are not lost at the gate.

What parents can usefully do

  • Keep the calendar. Put school test dates, project deadlines, practicals and pre-boards on one family calendar.
  • Watch internal assessment. Ask every few weeks whether lab records, projects and portfolio work are up to date.
  • Look at test papers, not just marks. The pattern of mistakes tells you whether the problem is concepts, speed or careless errors.
  • Protect the routine. Limit late family events and screen time in exam months, and keep meals and sleep regular.
  • Keep perspective. Encourage effort and steady habits. Comparing your child with cousins or classmates rarely helps.

When a tutor helps

Not every Class 10 student needs a tutor. A tutor is worth considering when:

  • marks in maths or science fall in the September exam and your child cannot say why;
  • chapters keep piling up because school is moving faster than your child can keep pace with;
  • your child understands in class but freezes on case-based or application questions;
  • written answers lose marks for missing steps, units or diagrams;
  • your child needs someone to set timed papers and check them against the marking scheme in the pre-board months.

Frequently asked questions

Will there be two CBSE Class 10 board exams in 2027?

CBSE introduced two board exams for Class 10 from 2026: a compulsory main exam and an optional second exam to improve performance in up to three subjects. CBSE's 2026-27 circulars refer to the 2027 main examination, but the 2027 dates have not been announced. Check cbse.gov.in for the official schedule and rules.

How much do internal assessment marks count?

In the main subjects, internal assessment is 20 marks out of 100 and the board paper is 80. The school awards these marks through tests, activities, portfolio and practical work across the year, so they depend on steady work from April.

Should my child take Maths Standard or Maths Basic?

Both are 80-mark, 3-hour papers. Basic has more questions on remembering and understanding; Standard has more application and analysis. Decide with the school, based on your child's comfort with problem-solving and whether they might take mathematics in Class 11, and check the current rules before the school finalises the List of Candidates.

When should we start sample papers?

Start chapter-wise past questions from about July, then move to full CBSE sample papers under timed conditions from November. In January, focus on reviewing mistakes rather than piling up new papers.

Is it too late to get a tutor after the pre-boards?

Not too late, but the goal changes. After the pre-boards, a tutor can help with timed practice, answer presentation and a few weak chapters. Building strong foundations takes longer, which is why April to September is the better time to start.
