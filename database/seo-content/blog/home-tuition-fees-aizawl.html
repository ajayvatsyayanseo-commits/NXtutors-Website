Step one: describe the need before anyone quotes

A clear request brings back quotes you can compare. Vague requests bring back rates that mean different things. Before you ask for tutors, write down five details:

  • The class and the board. MBSE, CBSE, ICSE or an international programme, and for Classes 11–12 the stream.
  • Each subject on its own line. A tutor strong in senior mathematics may not take chemistry, and a separate line lets each tutor say what they teach well.
  • The goal. Keeping pace with school, recovering weak chapters, board revision, or an entrance test such as JEE or NEET alongside the board syllabus.
  • Where the class happens. The locality, the building, the floor and the entrance the tutor should use.
  • The days that suit your child. After school on weekdays, a weekend morning, or a mix.

Step three: decide the hours, then compare

The total you pay depends mostly on the hours you book. A small gap between two hourly rates matters less than booking one class too many or too few each week. Think about hours by stage:

  • Classes 1–5: short, regular visits for reading, writing, number work and homework habits. Reduce them once the child manages homework without help for a few weeks.
  • Classes 6–8: mathematics and science foundations, plus English where written answers are weak. Reduce them when school tests stop showing the same gaps.
  • Classes 9–10: board-syllabus practice and full written answers. Add time when practice papers keep failing on the same chapters.
  • Classes 11–12: core subjects of the chosen stream, sometimes split between two tutors or two weekly slots. Rethink the plan if the tutor is reteaching school lessons instead of moving beyond them.
  • Entrance preparation: board chapters and entrance problems planned together. If mock-test errors gather in one subject, a specialist for that subject may help more than extra hours overall.

Fix the length of each class before the first paid one. When a class creeps from one hour towards two, the bill grows without anyone deciding it.

Step four: plan an HSLC or HSSLC year early

The Mizoram Board of School Education (MBSE) holds the HSLC examination for Class 10 students and the HSSLC examination for Class 12 students, and its office is in Aizawl. Its website, mbse.edu.in, carries exam notices, forms and results. Check dates and current rules there rather than relying on what was true in a previous session.

Rarely does a board year change a tutor's rate. It changes the number of hours, and the timing of those hours. Agree three things at the start of the session:

  1. The weekly pattern for the first term, while the class finishes any weak chapters from the year before.
  2. Who sets and marks practice papers in the middle of the year, and how often.
  3. How many extra revision classes are planned ahead of the board papers, and at what rate.

Wet months and online classes

Heavy rain is common in Aizawl between April and October, and wet evenings slow every journey on hill roads. Agree a rain rule before the season begins: perhaps the weekly maths class is taught on screen through those months, or a home class turns into an online one, with notice, when the rain is very heavy. Decide too whether the online rate matches the home rate, so nobody has to settle it at the last moment. Read home tutor or online tutor? to judge which subjects work well on a screen.

Write the agreement in one message

A short message that the tutor replies to is enough. Cover:

  • the unit of the fee, hourly, per class or monthly, and for a monthly fee the number of classes included;
  • the days, start times and class length, written as times;
  • the address, building, floor and entrance, and whether the quote includes the tutor's travel;
  • notice for cancellations on either side, and when missed classes are made up;
  • the rain rule and the rate for online classes;
  • the revision plan for the months before board papers;
  • a date, such as the end of a term, to look back at progress and decide what comes next.

Is tuition for an HSSLC year charged at a higher rate?

Not as a rule. The class, subjects and goal decide the rate. What a board year usually changes is the number of hours, which tends to rise in the last months before the papers. Settle early how many revision classes you want and at what rate, so nothing about the plan surprises you later.
