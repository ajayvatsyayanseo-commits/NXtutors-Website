Home or online is usually the first decision parents make about tuition, and it is often made by habit: "we always had a tutor at home" or "online is cheaper". Neither is a good reason on its own. The right choice depends on your child's age, the subject, the board, the goal, and how well your child concentrates without an adult in the room.

This guide gives you an honest comparison, factor by factor, with a table you can scan in a minute, a hybrid option that suits many families, and a simple decision guide at the end.

By age and class

Classes 6–8. Younger children usually do better with a tutor in the room. The tutor can see their notebook, correct how they set out working, and keep them on task. Online classes can work at this age, but usually only if a parent is nearby and the child is comfortable on a screen.

Classes 9–10. Either format can work. This is when board exam habits start: neat working, full steps, timed practice. If your child tends to drift, home tuition helps. If they are self-driven, online opens up a wider choice of tutors.

Classes 11–12. Older students are generally more able to manage online classes, and the subjects become more specialised, so a wider pool of tutors matters more.

By subject

Maths and numerical physics or chemistry. These depend on seeing the student's working line by line. At home, the tutor watches the pen. Online, this works well only with a proper setup: a shared digital whiteboard, a writing tablet, or a phone camera pointed at the notebook. Without that, the tutor sees only the final answer.

Theory-heavy subjects such as biology, social science, economics and English literature adapt well to online classes, since much of the work is discussion, explanation and written answers that can be shared as photos or documents.

By board and goal

CBSE and ICSE board exams. Tutors for these boards are widely available in most cities, so both formats are realistic. Pick based on your child's attention and schedule.

JEE and NEET. The goal is high-volume problem-solving and doubt clearing alongside coaching. Online suits many students because it fits around coaching timings and removes travel. Home tuition suits students who need someone to sit with them through long problem sets.

IB and IGCSE. Tutors who know these syllabuses well, including internal assessments and specific paper styles, are fewer in any one area. Online widens your choice considerably. If you find a strong IB or IGCSE tutor near you, home tuition is a good option too.

Attention and discipline

Be honest about your child. If they switch tabs, keep their camera off or check their phone during online school, an online tutor will face the same problem. A tutor in the room removes most of those temptations.

If you choose online, set some rules: camera on, phone in another room, a desk rather than a bed, and a parent within earshot for younger children.

Travel and safety

Home tuition means no travel for your child, but a tutor visits your home. Ask for tutors who have been checked, and keep a parent or another adult at home during classes, especially for younger children. On NXTutors, verified tutors are checked by a person and are shown first; sample profiles on the site are clearly marked as samples.

Online tuition removes travel for everyone and suits late evening slots. The safety questions are different: use a shared family device in a common room, and keep class communication on channels a parent can see.

Cost patterns

On NXTutors, home tuition fees are typically about ₹800–₹2,500 per hour, depending on class, subject and the tutor's experience. Senior classes and international boards can cost more.

Online tuition removes the tutor's travel, which can make it cheaper in some cases, but not always: an experienced IB or JEE tutor may charge similar rates in either format. Compare the actual hourly quotes you receive, and include your own costs, such as a tablet or a reliable internet connection for online, or the time a parent needs to be at home for home tuition.

Schedule flexibility

Online is more flexible: no traffic delays, and short doubt-clearing sessions before a test are practical. Home tuition works best on fixed, regular slots, because rescheduling means reorganising the tutor's travel too.

Tech needs for online classes

  • A laptop or tablet; a phone screen is too small for most maths and science classes.
  • A stable internet connection, with a backup such as a phone hotspot.
  • Headphones with a microphone.
  • For maths and science: a writing tablet, a stylus, or a phone stand to show the notebook.

The hybrid option

Many families do not have to choose one. Common patterns that work:

  • Home for teaching, online for doubts. A weekly home class for new topics, plus a short online session before tests.
  • Home during term, online during exams or travel. Keeps continuity when schedules change.
  • AI help between classes. TutorTwin, an AI tutor on WhatsApp, gives step-by-step explanations for homework doubts at any hour, and your child takes the tricky questions to the next class. It is an AI and can make mistakes, so important answers should be checked with the tutor.

If you try a hybrid, ask whether the same tutor can teach both ways. Continuity usually matters more than the format.

A simple decision guide

  1. Is your child in Class 8 or below, or easily distracted? Start with a home tutor.
  2. Is the subject maths or numerical science? Home, or online only with a proper writing setup.
  3. Is it IB, IGCSE or a specialised exam, and are there few good tutors nearby? Go online.
  4. Is the schedule packed with school and coaching? Online, or a hybrid.

Frequently asked questions

Is online tuition as effective as home tuition?

It can be, for a student who concentrates well and has the right setup. Younger or easily distracted children usually do better at home.

Is online tuition cheaper?

Sometimes, because the tutor does not travel, but not always. Compare the actual quotes you receive.

Can maths be taught well online?

Yes, if the tutor can see your child's working. A digital whiteboard, a writing tablet or a phone camera over the notebook makes a big difference. Without one, home tuition is usually better for maths.
