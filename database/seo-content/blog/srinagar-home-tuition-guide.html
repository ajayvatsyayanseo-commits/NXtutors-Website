Srinagar grew along the Jhelum and around its lakes, so the city reads less like a grid and more like a set of neighbourhoods joined by bridges, a bypass and a flyover. Lal Chowk sits at the centre. Newer housing spreads south-west along the bypass and south towards Nowgam, and the older northern quarters gather around Anchar Lake and Khushal Sar. Where your home sits decides how a tutor reaches it and which weekly slot tends to hold.

The five NXTutors zones follow, locality by locality.

How do tutors get around Srinagar?

By road, on a two-wheeler or by auto and shared ride. Three routes matter most. The four-lane city bypass runs through Pantha Chowk, Sanat Nagar, Hyderpora, Tengpora and Bemina, linking the south-western colonies. The Jehangir Chowk–Rambagh flyover has ramps at Gogji Bagh, Natipora and Barzulla, giving a quick link between the centre and the south. And Srinagar railway station in Nowgam, on the Jammu–Baramulla line, lets a tutor from the Budgam or Pampore side arrive by train and finish the trip by road.

Civil Lines

The central zone along the Jhelum, from the business district to the planned plots of Jawahar Nagar.

  • Lal Chowk: the city's main business area since the early twentieth century. Late afternoon and evening classes, after offices thin out, are easier to reach.
  • Rajbagh: a riverside residential locality whose residents often give a smaller part as the address, such as Pathan Bagh, Kursoo Rajbagh, Rajbagh Extension or Aramwari. Name the part and a landmark, and fix a slot after the school-time rush on the main roads.
  • Jawahar Nagar: one of the city's two government-planned residential areas, with wide roads, parks and houses on equal-sized plots. Tutors come straight to the door.
  • Sonwar: also called Sonwar Bagh, running parallel to the Jhelum and made of smaller parts such as Old Sonwar Mohalla, Palpora and Banumsora. The main road is crowded at school times and in the evening; the inner colonies are quieter and easy to reach on foot.

Karan Nagar & Bemina

  • Karan Nagar: an older planned area whose Deewan Bagh part was declared the first civil colony in 1942. Daytime visitor traffic to the big institutions here is heavy, so allow extra time for after-school classes.
  • Batamaloo: an old locality west of Lal Chowk, long known for the city's main bus stop before it moved to Qamarwari. Shops and workshops line the main roads with homes in the lanes behind, so a slot after the morning market rush is easiest for a first demo.
  • Bemina: a large residential area built up for housing in planned colonies. Visits start at the front door, with room to park nearby. The bypass is heavy at peak hours, so mid-afternoon or later evening slots travel more smoothly.

Airport Road

The south-western suburbs on the way to the airport, which lies just beyond the city in Budgam district.

  • Hyderpora: grown from a village on the edge of the city into a busy suburb of independent houses and plots, known locally for its many coaching centres.
  • Peerbagh: a residential locality next to Hyderpora on the way towards Humhama, with a planned cooperative colony among its neighbours. Tutors reach the inner colonies by the side lanes.
  • Sanat Nagar: family houses in colony lanes, with shops on the main road. Sitting on the bypass makes it one of the easier southern localities to reach from several directions outside the rush.
  • Rawalpora: also known as Rawalpora Colony, with houses in colony lanes and no gate formalities. A small bridge beside the railway bridge over the Doodhganga links it with Bagh-e-Mehtab, handy for tutors coming from the Nowgam side.

Natipora & Nowgam

  • Natipora: family houses in lanes off the main road, with Baghat, Mini Colony and Lal Nagar around it. Its flyover ramp, open since 2018, gives tutors from Lal Chowk or Jawahar Nagar a quick way in; evening classes after the rush below are popular.
  • Barzulla: the southern end of the flyover, with its ramp near the weather station. Leave a little extra time for after-school slots.
  • Nowgam: home to Srinagar railway station, opened in 2008. Houses sit in colony lanes. A tutor from the Budgam or Pampore side can take the train and a short ride onward.
  • Bagh-e-Mehtab: a residential suburb on the Doodhganga, once an orchard, now a government housing colony and several private colonies on the road towards Chadoora.

North City

The older north of Srinagar, between Anchar Lake, Khushal Sar and Dal Lake, with newer colonies pushing north towards the city's edge.

  • Soura: an old settlement east of Anchar Lake near the southern end of the 90 Feet Road. Large institutional grounds keep nearby roads busy all day, so tutors use the inner lanes and late afternoon slots.
  • Buchpora: former farmland on the eastern side of Anchar Lake, now residential colonies of newer houses. Through traffic towards Ganderbal builds at peak hours, so tutors from the centre tend to choose afternoons.
  • Lal Bazar: a residential area made of named neighbourhoods such as Mughal Street, Broadway Street, Rose Lane, Sikh Bagh and Bagwanpora. The main roads are lively at school times, so classes work once children are home in the afternoon.
  • Zadibal: an old locality on the eastern banks of Khushal Sar, long known for shawl weaving and papier-mâché work. Close lanes mean cars may not reach every door; tutors park on the main road and walk.
  • Nowshera: laid out in the fifteenth century. Traditional houses fill the old lanes and newer homes the wider roads.
  • Hazratbal: on the north-western shore of Dal Lake, with neighbourhoods such as Naseem Bagh, Zakura and Habak. Many students and teachers from nearby higher-education institutions live here, so ask for a senior-class subject tutor who lives close by. Roads near the lake front can be much busier at times, so ask the tutor which route they use before a first demo.

Winter, boards and timing across Srinagar

The long winter break and the short days of December and January are the main timing question everywhere in the city. Most families move home visits to late morning or early afternoon for those months, keep a few classes online on the coldest days, or use the break for a revision block before exams.

Schools here follow JKBOSE (the Jammu and Kashmir Board of School Education), CBSE or the CISCE's ICSE and ISC, and a few families look for IB or IGCSE support. Mention the board, plus the language of the exam papers for JKBOSE students, so each matched tutor explains in a way that fits. For the board's calendar and notices, use its official website. Frequently asked questions

Which routes do home tutors use to cross Srinagar?

Mostly the city bypass for the south-western colonies, the Jehangir Chowk–Rambagh flyover between the centre and Natipora or Barzulla, and the central roads around Lal Chowk. Tutors from the Budgam or Pampore side can also take the train to Srinagar station in Nowgam and finish by road.

Which parts of Srinagar have narrow lanes a car cannot reach?

The older northern quarters, especially Zadibal and the old lanes of Nowshera, where homes sit close together. Tutors there usually park on the main road and walk the last stretch, so share a clear landmark and the lane name, and expect the tutor to arrive on foot.

When should classes run during the winter months?

December and January bring short, cold days and the long winter break, so many families move classes to late morning or early afternoon, or switch some of them online. Decide the winter plan with the tutor in autumn, and confirm whether the class length and rate stay the same.

We live in Bemina. What should we tell a new tutor?

Give the colony name you use as your address, a nearby landmark and the lane. Homes there are mostly colony houses, so the tutor comes straight to the door. Bemina sits on the busy bypass, so choose a mid-afternoon or later evening slot rather than the peak rush.

Can families in Buchpora or Nowgam get a home tutor?

Yes, the request works the same way. NXTutors searches outward from your home: nearby colonies first, then the wider zone and the rest of the city, and then online tutors for any specialist subject. Someone living in an adjoining colony usually keeps a fixed weekly slot most reliably.
