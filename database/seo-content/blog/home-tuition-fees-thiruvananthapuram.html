Step one: how many hours does your child need?

Does the board change the fee?

Thiruvananthapuram families study under several boards: the Kerala State Board, which follows the state syllabus with the SSLC at the end of Class 10 and the Higher Secondary course, often called Plus Two, in Classes 11 and 12; CBSE; ICSE and ISC; and the international IB and IGCSE programmes. The board rarely sets a rate on its own. What moves a quote is how much board-specific experience the tutor brings and how senior the class is.

  • Kerala State Board, CBSE and ICSE up to Class 10: many tutors teach more than one of these, which gives you more quotes to compare.
  • Higher Secondary, ISC and CBSE Classes 11–12: subject depth matters more, so families often use a separate tutor for each science subject, and quotes sit higher in the band.
  • IB and IGCSE: the assessment style is different, and fewer tutors know it well, so expect quotes toward the upper end and consider an online specialist.

If your child is moving between boards, for example from the state syllabus to CBSE in Class 11, budget a few weeks of catch-up hours from the third row of the first table.

Five lines to agree before the first paid class

  1. The weekly hours and the day. Write down the slot, so both sides plan around it.
  2. Cancellations. What happens if the family or the tutor cancels, and whether a missed class is made up.
  3. Exam-season hours. Whether extra classes before the SSLC, board or entrance exams are at the same rate.
  4. Payment timing. Monthly in advance, or after the classes are held.
  5. A review point. A date, such as after the next unit test, to decide whether the hours go up, down or stay the same.
