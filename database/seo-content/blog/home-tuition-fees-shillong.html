Start with your own six questions

Before comparing anyone's quote, write down your answers to these. They make every conversation with a tutor shorter and every quote easier to compare.

  1. What is the problem we want solved? Homework that never gets finished, one weak subject, a board paper, or an entrance test alongside school.
  2. Which subjects, in which order of urgency? One subject handled well beats three handled thinly.
  3. How long can the child concentrate? A Class 3 child and a Class 12 student need very different class lengths.
  4. Which evenings are actually free? School buses, games and family routines leave fewer open slots than a timetable suggests.
  5. Home, online or a mix? Shillong has no railway, so every home visit depends on the road, and online can carry part of the load.
  6. When will we review it? A unit test or the end of a term gives a natural point to decide whether to continue.

Hours matter more than small rate differences

The number of hours you book each week decides the bill far more than a gap between two quotes, so plan the hours first. A useful rule is to start with the fewest hours that cover the real problem, then add time only when a test shows it is needed.

  • Classes 1–5: short, regular visits that build reading, writing and number habits. Stop or reduce once homework is managed alone.
  • Classes 6–8: the base for later maths and science, plus English if written answers lose marks. Review after two unit tests.
  • Classes 9–10: steady work through the syllabus, then more answer-writing and practice papers as the board year moves on.
  • Classes 11–12: separate time for each core subject of the stream, so one subject does not swallow the others.
  • Entrance preparation: school chapters and JEE or NEET practice in a single plan; if one subject keeps losing marks, consider a separate specialist for it.

SSLC, HSSLC or CBSE: how the board year shapes the plan

Many Shillong students study under the Meghalaya Board of School Education (MBOSE), which conducts the SSLC examination for Class 10 and the HSSLC examinations for Class 12, while other families choose CBSE, ICSE, IB or IGCSE. Rain, early dark and the school calendar

Shillong's monsoon brings heavy rain, and winter evenings grow dark early. Neither alters what a tutor charges, but both affect how many classes actually happen. A workable arrangement is to decide on day one that a class blocked by very wet weather runs on a video call at its normal time, and that anything still missed is rescheduled before the month ends. Also decide whether a video class is billed like a home visit. If you are unsure which subjects work on screen, read home tutor or online tutor: how to choose.

Per class or by the month?

Some tutors count classes; others prefer a monthly arrangement. Either can work if it is clear. If you agree a monthly basis, write down how many classes the month includes, what happens in a month with a school holiday or exam break, and whether unused classes carry forward. If you pay per class, agree how much notice either side gives to cancel.

Put the terms in one short message

A message the tutor replies to is enough. It should name the days and start times, the class length, the address with a landmark and where a taxi can stop, the cancellation rule, the rain-day rule, the board-year revision plan, and a review date. Keeping it in one place helps both sides remember what was agreed.
