Start with the reason, not the rate

Before comparing anyone, write one sentence on what tuition should fix. It keeps quotes comparable and hours purposeful. Most requests fall into one of these:

  • Keeping up with school. Homework support and regular revision across the year, usually for younger classes or one weak subject.
  • Repairing a gap. A child who lost the thread in fractions, algebra or basic chemistry needs a tutor who can go back a class or two before moving forward.
  • A board year. Steady preparation for the Madhyamik at the end of Class 10 or the Higher Secondary examination at the end of Class 12, or the CBSE board papers.
  • An entrance goal. JEE or NEET preparation alongside Classes 11–12, which needs a tutor who has taught both kinds of question.

The first two need fewer, shorter visits; the last two need more hours and senior-class experience.

How many hours does a child actually need?

Weekly hours matter more to the total than a modest difference in rates. Begin with the fewest hours that serve the reason you wrote down, and add only when a test shows the need.

Madhyamik and Higher Secondary years in Tripura

The Tripura Board of Secondary Education (TBSE) was set up in 1973 under a state Act. It holds the Madhyamik examination after Class 10 and the Higher Secondary (+2 Stage) examination after Class 12, and publishes the syllabus for Classes 9 to 12 on its official website, tbse.tripura.gov.in. The board's notices also refer to Mathematics (Standard) and Mathematics (Basic) at Madhyamik level, so tell the tutor which one your child is taking. For dates and the current syllabus, check the official site rather than relying on an earlier year.

In a board year the rate stays put; the plan is what moves. Ask the tutor three things early:

  1. How many classes a week through the first term, while the school is still covering new chapters?
  2. Will practice papers be set, timed and corrected during the year?
  3. How many extra revision classes are planned before the board papers, and do they carry the usual rate?

Rain, puja holidays and exam leave

Three times of year interrupt a weekly routine in Agartala. Heavy monsoon evenings slow every journey on two wheels. The puja holidays in autumn change school timetables and family plans. And in the weeks before board papers, a student may want more classes, not fewer. Decide at the start whether a class in those weeks goes online, is moved to another day or is dropped, and the notice expected. Read home tutor or online tutor to judge which subjects suit a screen.

One tutor for everything, or one per subject?

Up to Class 8, a single tutor across subjects keeps homework in one place. From Class 9, and above all in Classes 11–12, a subject specialist usually serves better, even if that means two tutors. Compare quotes subject by subject.

Six points to settle in writing

  1. Fee basis: hourly, per visit or monthly, and how many visits a monthly fee covers.
  2. Schedule: weekdays and start and end times, written as clock times.
  3. Directions: the address, the nearest chowmuhani and where a two-wheeler can stand.
  4. Missed visits: notice from either side and a make-up rule.
  5. Screen classes: which days may switch to online and at what rate.
  6. Check-in: a test or term end at which the plan is reviewed.
