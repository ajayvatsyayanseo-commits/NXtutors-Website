JEE Main and JEE Advanced in brief

JEE (Main) is conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA). For 2026, Paper 1 (B.E./B.Tech.) was held in two sessions, in January and April, as a computer-based test. It had three subjects, mathematics, physics and chemistry, each with 20 multiple-choice questions in Section A and 5 numerical-value questions in Section B: 75 questions and 300 marks in total, over three hours. Both sections carried +4 for a correct answer and −1 for an incorrect one. Students could appear in one or both sessions, and the better NTA score counted. JEE (Main) results are used for admission to NITs, IIITs and other institutions, and JEE (Main) is also the qualifying test for JEE (Advanced).

JEE (Advanced) is the exam for admission to the IITs. It has two compulsory papers, taken on the same day, in computer-based mode. For 2026, candidates had to be among the top 2,50,000 successful candidates in JEE (Main), across all categories, to be eligible to register.

The pattern, eligibility rules and dates are set afresh for each year. The 2027 information bulletins have not been released at the time of writing, so check jeemain.nta.nic.in and jeeadv.ac.in for the current bulletin before you plan around any detail.

The three routes

1. A coaching centre

Coaching gives structure: a fixed syllabus plan, regular tests, a peer group and a clear pace. For a motivated student who keeps up, that structure is valuable. The limits are the flip side of the same thing. The batch moves at one speed, doubt time per student is limited, and a student who falls behind in one chapter often stays behind, because the next chapter starts on schedule.

2. A one-to-one tutor, at home or online

A one-to-one tutor works at your child's pace and starts from their actual gaps. Every minute is about one student. The limits: there is no built-in peer group, test series or ranking, and the quality of the plan depends entirely on the tutor. A single tutor covering all three subjects to JEE Advanced level is rare; most students who go this route use separate tutors for maths, physics and chemistry.

3. Both: coaching plus a tutor

In the hybrid route, coaching provides the syllabus plan, test series and peer benchmark, and a tutor handles the gaps: doubts that pile up after class, weak chapters, and analysis of test mistakes. This is often the most effective route for students who are doing reasonably well in coaching but not converting that into test scores. It is also the most demanding on time, so it needs careful planning.

Class 11 and 12 board work

JEE preparation happens alongside school, and board marks still matter: for many admissions, and because falling behind in school creates stress that spills into JEE work. The good news is that much of the JEE syllabus overlaps with Class 11 and 12 physics, chemistry and maths. The differences are in depth and style. JEE asks for multi-step problem-solving under time pressure; board papers reward complete, well-presented answers and cover some topics and question types that JEE does not emphasise.

Plan for this explicitly. A common pattern is to keep JEE-level problem-solving as the main work through most of the year, then shift weight to board-style writing practice in the weeks before school exams and pre-boards. A one-to-one tutor is particularly useful here, because they can switch between JEE depth and board presentation for the same chapter. Travel time in Gurgaon is a real cost

In Gurgaon, the time spent getting to and from coaching is often underestimated. A centre that looks close on a map can mean a long ride at evening peak hours, especially across the main arterial roads or along the expressways. Multiply that by several days a week, across two years, and it adds up to a large block of time that could have gone into practice or sleep.

When comparing options, count door-to-door time at the actual class hour, not the distance. A coaching centre within your zone, a hybrid with fewer coaching days, or online classes can all give time back. That time only helps if it is used well, so pair any saved hours with a clear weekly plan.

Signs a student needs a one-to-one tutor alongside coaching

  • Coaching test scores stay flat for several tests in a row, despite effort.
  • One subject lags well behind the other two.
  • Doubts pile up because there is no time to ask them in class.
  • The student understands solutions when shown, but cannot start problems alone.
  • Coaching homework and school work together feel unmanageable.
  • Board marks in school are falling while JEE work continues.
  • The student has gaps from Class 9 or 10 maths or science that the batch assumes are solid.

One or two of these signs is common. Several together usually mean the student needs individual help, often in just one subject.

How to use a tutor well

Keep a doubt list. Every day, write down questions from coaching and homework that could not be solved, with the source and the step where the student got stuck. Bring the list to each tutor session. This turns the tutor's time into targeted work rather than re-teaching.

Analyse every mock. After each test, sort mistakes into types: concept not known, concept known but misapplied, calculation slip, misread question, ran out of time. The tutor can then work on the right problem. A student losing marks to calculation slips needs different help from one who does not understand rotational motion.

Work chapter by chapter. Use the tutor for the chapters where tests show the biggest losses, rather than covering everything. Our topic-wise guides for JEE maths, JEE physics and JEE chemistry are a useful map.

Coordinate with coaching. Share the coaching schedule with the tutor so they reinforce what is being taught this week, not something unrelated.

Review monthly. Agree a short review each month: which chapters improved, what the test data shows, and what to focus on next.

A Class 11 to Class 12 timeline

Most schools in Gurgaon begin the new session in April. This outline follows that calendar; adjust it to your school and to the dates in the current NTA bulletin.

  1. Class 11, first months: Settle into the new pace. Fix any gaps from Class 10 maths and science early. Choose the route: coaching, tutor or hybrid.
  2. Class 11, middle of the year: Build core chapters thoroughly. Start a doubt list habit and a basic error log from tests.
  3. Class 11, school exams: Shift some weight to board-style answers for school tests. Do not let JEE work crowd out school entirely.
  4. Class 11, end of year: Revise weak Class 11 chapters. Many Class 12 topics build on them, so this revision pays off later.
  5. Class 12, first half: Cover Class 12 chapters while keeping Class 11 material alive through mixed practice. Increase full-length mock tests.
  6. Class 12, before JEE Main: Timed mocks, mistake analysis and revision of high-loss chapters. Check the current bulletin for session dates and plan around them.
  7. Class 12, around board exams: Board-style writing practice and sample papers, then back to JEE-style practice as the timetable allows.
  8. If aiming for JEE Advanced: Deeper, multi-concept problems and past Advanced papers, with a tutor for the subject that loses the most marks.

Frequently asked questions

Can a student crack JEE without coaching?

Some students prepare with one-to-one tutors and self-study instead of coaching. It needs a strong plan, a separate source of timed mock tests, and usually a tutor for each subject. It suits disciplined students, or those whose schedule or travel makes coaching impractical.

When should a JEE student start with a tutor?

Ideally as soon as a gap shows, whether that is in Class 11 or later. Fixing a weak chapter early is much easier than fixing it during Class 12 revision.

Home tutor or online tutor for JEE?

Both work. Online fits well around coaching timings and removes travel. A home tutor suits students who need someone to sit with them through long problem sets. For maths and physics online, make sure the tutor can see the student's working.

How many hours a week of tuition does a JEE student need?

It depends on the gap. A student in coaching who needs help in one subject may need two sessions a week. Students without coaching need more, across all three subjects. Review the plan every month using test data.

Will a tutor also help with board exams?

Yes, a one-to-one tutor can switch between JEE-level problems and board-style answers for the same chapter. Tell the tutor your board and school exam dates at the start.
