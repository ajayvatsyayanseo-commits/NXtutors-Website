South Chennai runs from the temple streets of Mylapore and the bazaars of T Nagar down through Velachery to Tambaram, and the way a tutor reaches a home changes a great deal along the way. Near the coast the MRTS carries most tutors. Through the middle, the suburban line to Tambaram and the Metro Blue Line do the work. In the newer southern suburbs, the last stretch is usually by two-wheeler, bus or auto.

This guide covers three zones: Adyar, Besant Nagar & Mylapore; T Nagar, Nungambakkam & Kodambakkam; and Velachery, Guindy & Tambaram. For each area it notes the station tutors use, whether a visit is a doorstep call or a gate sign-in, and when to time a weekday class.

Adyar, Besant Nagar & Mylapore

The MRTS opened from Chennai Beach to Chepauk on 16 November 1995 and reached Thirumayilai on 19 October 1997. The section on to Thiruvanmiyur, adding Mandaveli, Kotturpuram, Kasturba Nagar and Indira Nagar, followed on 26 January 2004. Teynampet on the Metro Blue Line opened on 25 May 2018. Metro Lines 3 and 4 are under construction through the zone, with stations planned at Thirumayilai, Adyar Junction, Alwarpet and Thiruvanmiyur.

  • Adyar: named nagars such as Kasturba Nagar, Indira Nagar and Venkatarathnam Nagar, where independent houses on leafy streets sit beside low-rise apartment blocks. Tutors on the MRTS finish by auto. Evening traffic builds at the Adyar signal on Lattice Bridge Road, so many families fix an early or late slot.
  • Besant Nagar: laid out by the Tamil Nadu Housing Board from the early 1970s into the early 1980s, with regular numbered streets and easy two-wheeler parking. There is no station inside; tutors use the MRTS in Adyar or Thiruvanmiyur, or the busy bus terminus. Weekday or morning slots avoid the weekend crowds near the beach.
  • Thiruvanmiyur: the East Coast Road begins here, and the OMR and Lattice Bridge Road pass through, which makes it a hinge between Adyar and the IT corridor. Apartment blocks ask visitors to sign in at the gate. Late afternoon beats the early-evening rush at the ECR and OMR junctions.
  • Mylapore: older houses on narrow lanes near the temple tank, and apartment buildings on tree-lined avenues. The lanes are tight for cars, so tutors come by two-wheeler or auto and walk the last stretch. Plan around the Panguni festival, when crowds fill the streets.
  • Alwarpet: mostly flats in small and mid-sized buildings, where the tutor gives a name to the watchman and parks on the street. Teynampet station and the MRTS stops in Mylapore are the nearest rail. Tutors coming from T Nagar or Nungambakkam often prefer an earlier slot.

T Nagar, Nungambakkam & Kodambakkam

Mambalam station dates from 1911, and the suburban South Line through Nungambakkam, Kodambakkam, Mambalam and Saidapet from 1931. Blue Line stations at Teynampet, Nandanam and Saidapet opened on 25 May 2018, and Thousand Lights on 10 February 2019. Ashok Nagar and Vadapalani on the Green Line, just west of the zone, opened on 29 June 2015. Metro Line 4 is under construction with stations at Nandanam, Kodambakkam and Kodambakkam Powerhouse.

  • T Nagar: laid out between 1923 and 1925, with streets radiating from a central park. Older houses and newer apartment buildings sit in residential pockets away from Pondy Bazaar and the textile and jewellery streets. Shopping crowds slow the bazaars at weekends and in festival seasons, so tutors park away from them.
  • Nungambakkam: apartments, builder-floor buildings and some houses behind the High Road. Buildings often keep a watchman register and have little visitor parking, so a two-wheeler or cab drop is simpler. Nungambakkam station is among the busiest in the city; late-afternoon or weekend slots avoid the office rush.
  • Kodambakkam: film studios and business premises beside residential streets, where older bungalows are giving way to apartment buildings. Houses mean a doorstep visit, flats a name at the gate. The flyover and main roads towards Vadapalani are congested in the evening.
  • West Mambalam: dense streets of houses, small apartment buildings and old temples. Car parking is hard, so most tutors arrive by two-wheeler, on foot from Mambalam station or by auto. Mid-afternoon or late-evening slots miss the station rush.
  • Saidapet: on the Adyar River, with older streets and weaving and dyeing workshops near the river, houses, flats and a few gated complexes that register visitors. Two metro stations and a suburban station serve it. Classes just after school hours avoid the busy river bridge.

Velachery, Guindy & Tambaram

Blue Line stations at Little Mount, Guindy, Alandur, Nanganallur Road and Meenambakkam opened on 21 September 2016, and St Thomas Mount on the Green Line on 14 October 2016. The MRTS reached Velachery on 19 November 2007 and, since 14 March 2026, runs on to Puzhuthivakkam, Adambakkam and St Thomas Mount. Metro Line 5 is under construction south of Alandur, with stations planned at Madipakkam, Medavakkam and Perumbakkam.

  • Guindy: homes sit in pockets between an industrial estate, a national park and institutional campuses, so share a landmark and gate details. Rail is easy, with Guindy on both the suburban line and the Blue Line. Kathipara junction is heavy at office hours.
  • Velachery: apartment complexes that register visitors, alongside independent houses. Vijayanagar junction is one of the busiest points in the city, so time classes just before or after the rush.
  • Madipakkam and Nanganallur: low-rise flats and houses around Madipakkam's lake, and a temple town of flats, builder floors and houses in Nanganallur. Puzhuthivakkam on the MRTS and Nanganallur Road on the Blue Line are the nearest rail. Avoid festival days near Nanganallur's temples.
  • Pallikaranai and Medavakkam: apartment complexes, gated communities, villas and plotted streets beside the marsh and along the road towards Tambaram. Neither has a station yet, so tutors come by road; gated communities may ask a resident to confirm the visitor.
  • Chromepet and Tambaram: apartments, independent houses and plotted layouts on the GST Road, both now under the Tambaram City Municipal Corporation. Chromepet and Tambaram stations on the suburban line make train plus auto the easy route. The GST Road is heavy at office hours.

When should a weekday class start?

  1. Soon after school. The Adyar signal, Nungambakkam High Road, Kathipara junction, Vijayanagar junction and the GST Road all fill up at office hours, so an early slot is the most likely to start on time.
  2. After the rush, near a station. Close to an MRTS, suburban or Blue Line station, a tutor who comes by train can manage a later evening slot.
  3. Weekday over weekend, near the beach and the bazaars. Besant Nagar's beachfront and T Nagar's shopping streets are busiest at weekends, so weekday classes run smoother there.

Frequently asked questions

Which MRTS stations serve Adyar and Mylapore?

Kasturba Nagar, Indira Nagar and Thiruvanmiyur serve Adyar, while Light House, Thirumayilai and Mandaveli run along Mylapore's eastern side. Tutors arriving by MRTS usually finish the trip by auto. Metro Lines 3 and 4 are under construction through both areas but carry no passengers, so the MRTS remains the rail route.

Do tutors need to register at the gate in Velachery and Pallikaranai?

Usually, yes. Apartment complexes in Velachery register visitors at the gate, and gated communities in Pallikaranai sometimes ask a resident to confirm first. Share the tutor's name and flat number before the free demo class, and add the tutor as a regular visitor so later classes start on time.

When is the easiest time for a home class in T Nagar?

A weekday slot at a quiet hour, away from the weekend and festival-season crowds around Pondy Bazaar and the textile and jewellery streets. Tutors aim for the residential streets and park away from the bazaars. Mambalam station and the T Nagar bus terminus give tutors a rail or bus option.

Is there a metro to Madipakkam or Medavakkam yet?

Not yet. Metro Line 5 is under construction with stations named Madipakkam, Medavakkam I and Medavakkam II, but it carries no passengers. For now tutors use Puzhuthivakkam or Velachery on the MRTS and finish by auto or bus, or come by two-wheeler from Velachery, Pallikaranai or the OMR side.
