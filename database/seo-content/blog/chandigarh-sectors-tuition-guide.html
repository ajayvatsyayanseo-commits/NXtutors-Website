Chandigarh's sectors look alike on a map, a neat grid of rectangles bounded by the same kind of roads, yet a home tutor's visit changes a good deal from one to the next. In the older northern sectors most families live in plotted houses and the tutor rings the bell at the gate. Further south, housing-board flats and cooperative group housing societies take over, and in Manimajra old-town lanes sit beside planned complexes. There is no metro yet, so the neighbouring sectors, the nearest market and the main road at office hours decide which tutors can reach you and which slot holds.

This guide covers the city's two zones, Sectors 1–30 and Sectors 31–56 with Manimajra, sector by sector.

How is the sector grid laid out?

Chandigarh was one of the earliest planned cities of independent India. Its plan uses standardised neighbourhood units called sectors and a seven-tier road system: the central axes are called Marg, such as Jan Marg, Madhya Marg and Dakshin Marg, and the arterial roads around sectors are called Path. Sectors 1 to 30 were developed in the first phase as low-rise plotted development, with Sector 17 as the central business district. Sectors 31 to 47 followed with four-storey apartments for government employees, at nearly four times the density, and apartment living took root with the cooperative group housing societies of Sectors 48 to 51.

The revived Chandigarh Metro, cleared in July 2024, is to be built in its first phase between 2027 and 2034. Until then the Chandigarh Transport Undertaking's buses from the inter-state bus terminals in Sectors 17 and 43 are the public-transport backbone for tutors.

Chandigarh Sectors 1–30

Sector 22 was the first sector to be developed, and the Capitol Complex in Sector 1 became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in July 2016. Most homes here are houses with their own gates, and the office rush on Madhya Marg is the main thing to plan around.

  • Sector 8: independent houses and low-rise blocks around a busy sector market, linked by Madhya Marg, Himalaya Marg, Sarovar Path and Vigyan Path. No society register to sign.
  • Sector 9: bungalows, houses and government residences on tree-lined streets between Sectors 8 and 10. Some government homes ask for a name at the entrance; elsewhere the tutor rings at the gate.
  • Sector 10: houses from one-kanal homes to duplexes, with the city's art gallery and a stretch of the Leisure Valley inside. The Rose Garden across Madhya Marg is a handy landmark.
  • Sector 11: builder floors and houses beside an education campus from the 1960s, next to Sectors 10 and 15. Builder floors often share one entrance, so give the floor and the bell.
  • Sector 15: beside the large campus in Sector 14, with a lively market and paying-guest homes among family houses. Many postgraduate students and researchers who tutor live or study close by.
  • Sector 16: the Rose Garden and the cricket stadium on Jan Marg, with houses and government housing between Madhya Marg and Jan Marg. On match days, move the class or hold it online.
  • Sector 18: independent houses next to Sectors 17 and 19, with a theatre for music and drama from the early 1960s as a landmark. The Sector 17 bus terminal makes it easy to reach by bus.
  • Sector 19: houses alongside housing-board flats, where a tutor walks up to the door. Sadar Bazaar in Sector 19-C on Sukhna Path draws evening crowds, so agree a parking spot early.
  • Sector 21: four blocks, A to D, of houses and builder floors, with a motor market, temples, a gurdwara and a post office. Dakshin Marg runs along its edge; share block, house number and floor.
  • Sector 22: a long-running street market and a large main market beside Sector 17, with houses and flats in calmer inner lanes. Evening parking is tight, so a tutor on bus or auto keeps better time.
  • Sector 27: houses and builder floors near Sectors 19 and 28, with the head post office for this side of the city. Chandi Path, Sukhna Path and Madhya Marg suit tutors from Panchkula or Manimajra.

Chandigarh Sectors 31–56 and Manimajra

Chandigarh Housing Board homes are spread across Sectors 38 to 41, 44 to 47, 51, 52 and 55 and the Modern Housing Complex at Mani Majra. The Terraced Garden in Sector 33 and the Garden of Fragrance in Sector 36 make good landmarks. Chandigarh Junction railway station lies on the eastern side and also serves Panchkula, and Housing Board Chowk is the key junction on Madhya Marg.

  • Sector 33: houses and builder floors near Sectors 32 and 34 and the old village of Burail. The Terraced Garden, laid out in 1979 with a musical fountain, is the landmark; Dakshin Marg is the road to time around.
  • Sector 35: a busy market of restaurants, boutiques and electronics shops, with houses and builder floors in quieter pockets. Tutors on public transport take a bus to the Sector 43 terminal and an auto.
  • Sector 36: plotted houses and floors on quiet streets around the Garden of Fragrance, laid out in 1998 around a lotus pond, with a large temple beside it. Visits are straight to the door.
  • Sector 38: houses and housing-board flats next to Sector 38 West, a separate pocket with its own market and small housing-board homes. Say which part you live in; most blocks have no staffed gate.
  • Sector 40: mid-budget housing-board flats and houses next to Sectors 39 and 41. The Sector 40-C market, known for its Sunday second-hand bike market, is the landmark, and tutors from Mohali's phases are within reach.
  • Sector 44: a housing-board sector split into 44-A, 44-C and 44-D, from one-room flats to larger upper and lower flats. The Sector 43 bus terminal is next door; share the sub-sector and block.
  • Sector 46: one of the few southern sectors with independent houses regularly available, beside housing-board flats, between Sectors 45 and 47. The roads towards Zirakpur and Mohali suit tutors from both towns.
  • Sector 49: mostly cooperative group housing society flats of two to four bedrooms, near Sectors 48, 50 and 51. Most societies have a guarded gate, so add the tutor's name to the visitor list once.
  • Manimajra: an old town founded in the early sixteenth century and notified as Sector 13 in February 2020. Old-town lanes with tight parking sit beside planned complexes that may ask for a name at the gate; Housing Board Chowk jams at rush hour.

Which weekday slots hold up in the city sectors?

  1. Soon after school. A class that begins before the evening traffic on Madhya Marg and Dakshin Marg is the most likely to start on time, especially for tutors from Panchkula, Mohali or Zirakpur.
  2. Later in the evening, with a nearby tutor. A tutor from the next sector, such as Sector 15 for Sector 11 or Sector 45 for Sector 46, can manage a slot after the rush.
  3. Weekend mornings for longer sessions, and online classes on stadium match days.

Frequently asked questions

Do home tutors in Chandigarh's sectors need a gate pass?

Rarely in the older sectors. In Sectors 1 to 30 most homes are plotted houses, so the tutor rings at the gate, though some government homes ask for a name. In the cooperative societies of Sectors 48 to 51 and planned complexes in Manimajra, register the tutor at the gate once.

How do tutors get around Chandigarh without a metro?

By car, scooter, city bus or auto. The Chandigarh Transport Undertaking runs buses from inter-state bus terminals in Sectors 17 and 43, and autos cover the last stretch. The revived metro is planned to be built in its first phase between 2027 and 2034, so it does not affect tutor travel yet.

Which roads should we plan class times around?

Madhya Marg, the main commuter corridor between Panchkula and Chandigarh, and Dakshin Marg south of the city centre are the busiest at office hours, with Housing Board Chowk the key junction near Manimajra. A class that starts before the evening rush, or with a tutor from the next sector, holds up well.

Is it easy to find a tutor in the southern housing-board sectors?

Usually, yes. Sectors such as 38, 40, 44 and 46 have many families in low-rise housing-board flats close together, and the Sector 43 bus terminal links them with the whole tricity. Sector 40 also sits on the Mohali side of the city, which brings tutors from Mohali's phases within reach.
