Does the board change what you pay?

Not as a rule. Surat students sit GSEB, CBSE, ICSE and ISC, and IB or Cambridge IGCSE, and each tutor sets one fee for their own reasons. What the board changes is who is suitable. For a GSEB student, tell us the medium of instruction, Gujarati, English or another, so the tutor uses the same terms as the textbook. For CBSE, ask how the tutor uses the NCERT books and sample papers. For IB and IGCSE, the tutor must know the assessment style, which is why those specialists sit toward the upper end.

How can you trim the bill without trimming the teaching?

  • Put the strongest tutor where the gap is. In Class 12, one weak subject deserves the specialist; a subject that needs only practice can go to a tutor lower in the band or to fewer sessions.
  • Move one subject online. A specialist for IB, IGCSE or JEE Advanced may not live near you; online removes the trip from the question. Our home versus online comparison helps decide which subject goes where.
  • Book fewer, longer sessions for senior classes. Two sessions of an hour and a half cover the same ground as three one-hour sessions with one trip fewer.
  • Agree festival weeks in advance. Navratri and the Diwali holidays change family routines. Settle beforehand how paused weeks are handled, so nobody pays for lessons that did not happen.
  • Cap exam-season extras. Extra classes before boards are common; agree the rate and a limit in advance.

Which questions should you ask a tutor about the fee?

  1. Is the fee per hour or per session, and how long is a session?
  2. Is it paid monthly in advance or after a set number of classes?
  3. What happens if we cancel, and what happens if you cancel?
  4. Are extra classes before exams charged at the same rate?
  5. How will we see progress, and how often?

There is no single right answer to any of these; what matters is agreeing before the first paid class. The NXTutors pricing guide sets out typical ranges by class and subject.
