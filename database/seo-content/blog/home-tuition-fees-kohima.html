One long class or two shorter ones?

Younger children usually learn more from two short visits than one long sitting, while senior students on long problems may prefer one longer class. At an hourly rate a longer class costs more each time, so fix the length before the first paid class rather than letting it drift.

How does an HSLC or HSSLC year change the plan?

The Nagaland Board of School Education (NBSE) conducts the HSLC examination at the end of Class 10 and the HSSLC examination at the end of Class 12, with HSSLC streams in arts, science and commerce. Its head office is at Upper Bayavü Hill in Kohima, and its official website, nbsenl.edu.in, carries notices, results and forms. For dates and the current pattern, rely on that site rather than on what a tutor remembers from an earlier year.

A board year changes the shape of tuition more than its hourly rate. Families usually think about it in three parts.

Does the board on its own change the rate?

Not much. A Kohima family may follow NBSE, CBSE, ICSE or an international programme. The board matters more for the questions you ask:

  • NBSE: ask which HSLC or HSSLC classes the tutor has taught recently and how they practise answer-writing for the board papers.

What about the monsoon months?

Rain in Kohima is heaviest from June to September, and wet evenings on hill roads slow every journey. Many families agree a rule with the tutor at the start: one class a week moves online during those months, or any class can switch online on a very wet day. Settle whether an online class carries the same rate as a home visit. Our comparison of home and online tutors helps decide which subjects travel well to a screen.

Which terms should you agree in writing?

A short message the tutor confirms is enough:

  1. How the fee is counted: per hour, per class or per month, and if monthly, how many classes it includes.
  2. Days and class length: written as times, not as "about an hour".
  3. Where the class happens: the address, the landmark and the nearest point a taxi can reach, and whether the tutor's travel is part of the quote.
  4. Cancellations: how much notice each side gives and when a missed class is made up.
  5. Rain and online days: when a class moves online and whether the rate changes.
  6. The board-year plan: the number of revision classes before HSLC, HSSLC or CBSE papers.
  7. A review date: a unit test or term end at which you decide together to continue, reduce or stop.
