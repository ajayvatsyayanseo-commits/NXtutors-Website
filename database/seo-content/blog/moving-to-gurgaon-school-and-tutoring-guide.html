If you are moving to Gurgaon (Gurugram) with school-age children, the most useful thing you can do is find out, before the move, exactly how your child's current board and syllabus differ from the new school's, and plan a short, focused catch-up in the subjects where the gap is real. For most families that means maths and one language, not everything. Start with online tutoring while you are still settling, then switch to a home tutor once you know where you will live and what the school timetable looks like.

This guide is for families relocating mid-year from another Indian city or from abroad. It covers what changes when you switch boards, a catch-up plan by class, second-language gaps, how to think about where to live versus the school commute, when to start a tutor, and why an online-first, home-later approach suits most moves.

What changes when you move boards?

Gurgaon has schools following CBSE, ICSE/ISC, the IB and Cambridge or Edexcel IGCSE, and a few following a state board. Your child may be moving within the same board, which is usually straightforward, or across boards, which needs more planning.

Content is rarely the biggest problem. The style of the questions and the way answers are marked cause more trouble in the first term. A student who was top of the class in one system can score lower in the first tests at a new school simply because they do not yet know what the examiner wants to see. Before you move: a checklist

  • Get the new school's syllabus and chapter plan for the current term, and ask which chapters have already been taught.
  • Ask for the test and exam calendar, especially any unit tests in the first four to six weeks after joining.
  • Bring your child's recent report cards, test papers and notebooks. A tutor can diagnose far more from a marked test than from a report card.
  • Ask about the second language and whether any exemption or alternative is possible for a student joining late.
  • Check the documents needed for admission from another board: transfer certificate, mark sheets and any board-level eligibility or equivalence paperwork. Schools will tell you what their board requires.
  • Ask about board-exam classes early. Moves into Class 10 or Class 12 are the hardest, because students are registered for board exams. Speak to the admissions office well in advance.

A catch-up plan by class

Classes 1 to 5

Young children adapt quickly. Focus on reading and writing fluency in English, basic number skills and the second language. A short weekly session with a patient tutor, plus reading at home, is usually enough. Do not try to "cover" every missed chapter; the school will revisit most basics.

Classes 6 to 8

This is when gaps start to compound in maths and science. Ask the school which chapters were taught before your child joined and plan to fill the ones that later topics build on: fractions, ratio, integers, algebraic expressions and simple equations in maths; and the basic science units the next chapters depend on. Two sessions a week for six to eight weeks is a common starting plan.

Classes 9 and 10

These years count towards board exams, so catch-up needs to be deliberate. Map every chapter of the board syllabus against what your child has already studied, mark the missing ones, and schedule them before the school's next major test. Practise board-style questions early, because the marking style differs between boards, and ask the school for its sample papers so your child sees the format before the first test.

Classes 11 and 12

The subjects are specialised, and a mid-year move can leave a gap in a chapter that later chapters depend on, such as functions and trigonometry in maths or mechanics in physics. Subject-specific tutors are usually needed rather than one tutor for everything. If your child is also preparing for an entrance exam, coordinate the catch-up with that timetable so neither suffers.

Second-language gaps

Language is the gap families most often underestimate. Hindi is widely taught as a second language in Gurgaon's CBSE and ICSE schools, and some schools also offer Sanskrit, French or other languages. A child coming from another state may have studied a different Indian language; a child coming from abroad may have studied none, or a European language at a different level.

  • Ask the school first. Some schools allow a late joiner to take a different language or start at a lower level; the rules depend on the school and the board.
  • Build reading and writing, not only speaking. Many children can speak Hindi comfortably but cannot yet read or write it at their class level.
  • Short and frequent works best. Three short sessions a week usually do more than one long one for a language.
  • Use the textbook the school uses. Language exams in Indian boards are closely tied to the prescribed readers and grammar books.

Where to live versus the school commute

Many families choose a school first and then look for a home, which is sensible, because the school run in Gurgaon can be long. Traffic builds sharply at school and office hours, and a short distance on the map can take a long time at 7.30 in the morning. Visit at those hours before you sign a lease.

When should you start a tutor?

For most moves, the right time is two to four weeks before your child joins the new school, or as soon as you know the new syllabus. That gives time to close the most urgent gaps before the first tests. If you are moving at very short notice, start in the first week after joining, once the teachers have told you where your child stands.

Signs you should not wait:

  • The first unit tests are within a month of joining.
  • Your child is moving across boards in Class 9 or above.
  • The school says your child has missed chapters that later topics depend on.
  • Your child is anxious about the new school or the language.

Online first, then home

In the first weeks after a move, you may be in temporary accommodation, the school timetable is unfamiliar, and you do not yet know which evenings are free. Online tutoring fits this stage well: it can start before you arrive, does not depend on your address, and gives you a wide choice of tutors who know the new board.

Once you have settled, a home tutor often works better, especially for younger children and for maths, where the tutor needs to watch the working. Frequently asked questions

When should we start tuition if we are moving to Gurgaon mid-year?

Ideally two to four weeks before your child joins the new school, once you have the new syllabus and chapter plan. That gives enough time to close the most urgent gaps before the first unit tests. If the move is sudden, start in the first week after joining, guided by what the teachers say.

Is it hard to switch from a state board to CBSE in Gurgaon?

Most students manage it well within a term. The main differences are usually the order of topics, the depth of maths and science, and the style of board questions. A short plan that maps the new syllabus against what your child has already studied, with focused practice on missing chapters, is usually enough.

My child has never studied Hindi. What can we do?

Ask the school first, because some schools allow late joiners a different language or a lower starting level. If Hindi is required, start with reading and writing using the school's own textbook, in three short sessions a week. Speaking often comes faster than writing, so track written work specifically.

Should we choose a home or an online tutor after moving?

Online usually works best in the first weeks, while you settle and learn the school timetable. Once you know your address and free evenings, a home tutor suits younger children and maths well. Many families keep the same tutor for both, which gives continuity through the move.

Which subjects need the most catch-up after a move?

Maths and the second language most often, because both build step by step and depend on earlier chapters. Science needs attention in Classes 9 to 12. English and social science usually settle through school work, unless your child is moving to ICSE, where English writing carries more weight.
