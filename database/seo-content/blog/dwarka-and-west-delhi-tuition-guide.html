In Dwarka most families live in cooperative group housing societies, so a home tutor's first visit starts at a society gate with a guard and a visitor register. Further east, in Janakpuri, Rajouri Garden, Punjabi Bagh and Karol Bagh, most homes are houses and builder floors, and the tutor comes straight to the door. Across all of it, the Blue Line is the spine: a tutor who lives near any of its stations can reach much of West Delhi by metro and finish by e-rickshaw.

This guide covers three zones: Dwarka; Janakpuri, Rajouri Garden and Punjabi Bagh; and Karol Bagh, Patel Nagar and Rajinder Nagar. It shows which station serves which area, the gate routine and when to time a class.

How do the three zones fit together?

The Blue Line section from Barakhamba Road to Dwarka opened on 31 December 2005, taking in Karol Bagh, Rajendra Place, Patel Nagar and Shadipur, then the stations along Najafgarh Road such as Rajouri Garden, Subhash Nagar, Tilak Nagar and Janakpuri, before reaching Dwarka. Three other lines cross it: the Green Line through Punjabi Bagh and Paschim Vihar, the Pink Line through Rajouri Garden and Naraina Vihar, and the Magenta Line from Janakpuri West towards Palam.

Dwarka: the sector grid

Dwarka is a sub-city developed by the Delhi Development Authority, organised into sectors, with cooperative group housing societies as the main residential option; its area is 5,648 hectares as per DDA records. The Blue Line reached Dwarka on 31 December 2005, was extended to Dwarka Sector 9 on 1 April 2006, and from Dwarka Sector 9 to Dwarka Sector 21 on 30 October 2010. Dwarka Sector 21 is an interchange with the Airport Express Line, which opened on 23 February 2011, and Yashobhoomi Dwarka Sector 25 station opened on 17 September 2023, extending the Airport Express westwards.

Sectors with a station inside

Sector 9, Sector 10, Sector 11, Sector 12, Sector 13 and Sector 14 each have their own Blue Line station, so a tutor from anywhere along the line can walk or take a short e-rickshaw ride to the society. Around the Sector 10 market, allow for the evening crowd when fixing an early-evening class.

Sectors without a station

  • Sector 4 and Sector 5: almost entirely society flats in the middle of the sub-city. Tutors use the Sector 10, 12 or 13 stations.
  • Sector 6 and Sector 7: on the eastern, Palam side, with the Blue Line at Sector 10 and the Magenta Line at Palam, so a tutor from Janakpuri can come on either line. Start the class a little before the evening rush towards Pankha Road.
  • Sector 19: apartment complexes beside the older lanes of Ambrahi village. Society flats have a guard; village homes open straight onto the lane.
  • Sector 22 and Sector 23: towards the airport, reached from the Sector 21 interchange. The Dwarka Expressway's Delhi section passes Sectors 21, 22, 25, 27 and 28, and its full route became operational in June 2025, so set classes a little after the office rush on the roads leading to it.

In every sector, share the tower and flat number, and ask the guard to note the tutor as a regular visitor; some societies ask residents to confirm each visit, so arrange standing permission in the first week.

Janakpuri, Rajouri Garden and Punjabi Bagh

Janakpuri West became an interchange between the Blue Line and the Magenta Line, which reached it on 29 May 2018. The Green Line opened from Inderlok to Mundka on 3 April 2010, serving Punjabi Bagh and Paschim Vihar, and its Kirti Nagar to Ashok Park Main branch opened on 27 August 2011. Punjabi Bagh West opened on 29 March 2022 as a Green and Pink Line interchange, and Naraina Vihar opened on 14 March 2018 with the Pink Line's first section.

  • Janakpuri and Vikaspuri: Janakpuri was planned in the late 1960s, in lettered blocks and pockets that make a first visit easy to find. Society flats keep a guard register; houses and floors are doorstep visits. Vikaspuri has no station inside, so tutors use the Najafgarh Road stations and an e-rickshaw.
  • Uttam Nagar: builder floors and houses served by four Blue Line stations: Uttam Nagar East, Uttam Nagar West, Nawada and Dwarka Mor. Share a clear landmark and the floor number.
  • Tilak Nagar, Subhash Nagar and Hari Nagar: DDA flats beside older floors and houses. Najafgarh Road is heavy in the evening, so a metro-friendly slot after school runs more reliably.
  • Rajouri Garden and Naraina Vihar: the Rajouri Garden interchange brings tutors in from many directions. Choose slots away from the busiest shopping hours around the main market.
  • Punjabi Bagh and Paschim Vihar: Punjabi Bagh was built on land acquired from Madipur village to resettle refugees from Pakistan; it was called Refugees Colony until it was renamed in 1960, and the Ring Road divides it into East and West. Pick a tutor on your side of the Ring Road, or one who comes by the Green or Pink Line.
  • Kirti Nagar and Moti Nagar: Moti Nagar was formed in 1948 to 1950 with help from the Delhi Improvement Trust as a resettlement colony. Kirti Nagar's furniture market is busy by day, so evening or weekend slots, once shops start closing, suit tutors who drive.

Karol Bagh, Patel Nagar and Rajinder Nagar

Karol Bagh, Rajendra Place, Patel Nagar and Shadipur stations opened on 31 December 2005 with the Barakhamba Road to Dwarka section. Karol Bagh took in villagers displaced by building work in the 1920s, and after Partition in 1947 many refugees from West Punjab and Sindh. Rajinder Nagar developed in the 1950s as a Punjabi refugee colony, and Patel Nagar was developed on land acquired from Shadipur, Khampur and neighbouring villages.

  • Karol Bagh: residential pockets set among busy market streets. Tutors usually come by metro and walk to the floor; weekday sessions are easier than weekends, when shopping crowds peak.
  • Old Rajinder Nagar and New Rajinder Nagar: a main road divides the two. Old Rajinder Nagar is one of Delhi's two main education hubs for government job aspirants, known especially for civil services coaching, so its lanes stay busy with students into the evening; give the exact floor and a landmark. New Rajinder Nagar's green blocks are calmer, and tutors walk from Rajendra Place.
  • East Patel Nagar and West Patel Nagar: mostly builder floors, so there is no society gate to clear. Rajendra Place suits East Patel Nagar; Patel Nagar and Shadipur serve the west. Avoid the office-closing hours if the tutor drives.

When should a weekday class start?

  1. Soon after school. Najafgarh Road, the Ring Road, the Outer Ring Road and the roads towards the Dwarka Expressway are busiest at office hours, so an early slot is the most likely to start on time.
  2. After the rush, near a station. In Dwarka sectors with their own station, and near Rajouri Garden, Kirti Nagar or Rajendra Place, a tutor who comes by metro can manage a later evening slot.
  3. Weekend mornings for longer sessions.

Frequently asked questions

Which Dwarka sectors have their own metro station?

On the Blue Line, Sectors 8 to 14 and Sector 21 have stations named after them, and Sector 21 is also the Airport Express interchange. Sectors such as 4, 5, 6, 7, 19, 22 and 23 have no station inside, so tutors come to a nearby station and finish by e-rickshaw.

Do tutors need a gate pass in Dwarka societies?

Usually, yes. Most Dwarka families live in cooperative group housing societies, where a guard keeps a visitor register and may call the flat before letting anyone in. Give the guard the tutor's name before the free demo class, and ask for standing permission for a regular tutor so later visits start on time.

Is it easier to find a tutor near a Blue Line station?

Often, yes. A home near a Blue Line station can be reached by tutors from anywhere along the line, from Dwarka to Janakpuri, Rajouri Garden and Karol Bagh, so the shortlist can draw from a wider area. Interchanges such as Janakpuri West, Rajouri Garden and Kirti Nagar widen it further.

When is the easiest time for a home class in West Delhi?

A slot soon after school, before the office rush on Najafgarh Road and the Ring Road, is the most reliable. Weekday classes suit Karol Bagh and Rajouri Garden better than weekends, when shopping crowds peak. Near a station, a tutor who comes by metro can also manage an evening slot after the rush.
