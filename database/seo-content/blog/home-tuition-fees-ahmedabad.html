Where does your child's stage and board sit in the band?

Class, board and subject set most of a fee. Ahmedabad families study across CBSE, ICSE and ISC, IB and IGCSE, and the Gujarat Secondary and Higher Secondary Education Board (GSEB), so the same tutor may quote differently for different students.

  • Classes 1 to 5: usually toward the lower part of the band. Homework support, reading and number work matter more than specialist depth, and one or two short classes a week are often enough.
  • Classes 6 to 8: still mostly in the lower and middle part. Maths and science start to need a tutor who explains rather than supervises, so ask each tutor how they check that a topic has been understood.
  • Classes 9 and 10: the middle of the band for CBSE, ICSE and GSEB. The board year brings more practice papers, so plan the hours in advance rather than adding them in a panic before exams.
  • GSEB students: tell us the medium of instruction. A tutor who can explain in the language the child studies in saves time at every class, and it is worth confirming this at the demo.
  • Classes 11 and 12 science: toward the upper end, and often one tutor per subject, since few tutors teach physics, chemistry and maths equally well at this level.
  • IB and IGCSE: toward the upper end, because the tutor must know the programme's assessment style. IB HL specialists can charge more than ₹2,500 an hour.
  • JEE and NEET: toward the upper end, and JEE Advanced specialists can charge more. Many families pair a home tutor for board subjects with online sessions for entrance practice.

Does the side of the Sabarmati change the fee?

Not the band. Where you live mainly decides how many tutors can reach you easily, which changes how many quotes you can compare. In Ahmedabad the river, two metro lines, the BRTS and a few main roads do most of that sorting. The metro's Blue Line runs east to west across the river, from Vastral Gam in the east to Thaltej Gam in the west, and the Red Line runs north to south on the west bank, from Motera Stadium to APMC in Vasna. The BRTS ran 18 routes with 197 stations as of March 2025.

The west bank along the metro

Navrangpura, Ellisbridge, Paldi, Ambawadi, Vasna, Naranpura and Sabarmati all have Red or Blue Line stations in or next to them, and the two lines meet at Old High Court. A family here can draw tutors from along either line, so the shortlist is usually wide. The West Ahmedabad tuition guide goes area by area.

The SG Highway belt and the western edge

Satellite, Jodhpur, Prahlad Nagar, Bopal, South Bopal, Shela and Gota have no metro station of their own, though Thaltej, Memnagar and parts of Bodakdev are close to the Blue Line's western end. Tutors mostly come by two-wheeler, car or BRTS, and SG Highway is slow at office hours, so a tutor who lives close by and can keep a late-afternoon slot is often worth more than a slightly lower rate from across the city.

The east bank

Vastral, Amraiwadi and Kankaria have Blue Line stations, while Maninagar, Naroda and Asarwa rely on railway stations and road links. In the older areas many homes are independent houses on busy lanes, so the tutor comes straight to the door, and an evening slot fixed away from market and shift traffic holds better. Two children at home: one tutor or two?

One tutor can sometimes teach both children. It can work when the two are close in class and board, and the tutor teaches one child after the other in the same visit, which saves a trip. It works less well when one child is in Class 4 and the other in Class 11 science, since few tutors are equally strong at both levels. The fee for each child is still set by the tutor, so ask each shortlisted tutor how they price two back-to-back sessions and compare the total, not only the hourly figure.

Which weeks of the year stretch the budget?

  • The run-up to school and board exams: extra classes are common. Agree in advance whether extra hours are at the same rate, and cap them.
  • The Navratri and Diwali weeks: many families pause or move classes. Agree how paused weeks are handled before they arrive, so nobody pays for classes that did not happen.
  • The summer break: a lighter plan of one or two classes a week, or online sessions if the family travels, keeps the habit without paying for a full timetable.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a home tutor cost in Ahmedabad?

Across NXTutors, most home-tuition sessions fall between ₹800 and ₹2,500 an hour; Classes 11–12, IB/IGCSE and JEE/NEET sit toward the upper end; specialists for IB HL or JEE Advanced can charge more. Each tutor sets their own fee, and you see it before the free demo class.

How much should we budget for three subjects in Class 12?

At two hours a week per subject, three subjects come to 24 hours in four weeks: ₹19,200 at ₹800 an hour and ₹60,000 at ₹2,500. Class 12 science usually sits toward the upper end, so plan nearer the higher figure, or move one subject online if the total is out of reach.

Is a GSEB tutor cheaper than a CBSE or ICSE tutor?

Not as a rule. Class, subject and the tutor's experience set most of a fee, and each tutor sets their own. For a GSEB student, the more useful check is whether the tutor can teach in the child's medium of instruction and knows the board's textbooks, which you can confirm at the free demo.

Does living near a metro station in Ahmedabad lower the fee?

Not directly. A home near a Red or Blue Line station can be reached by tutors from further along the line, so you may get more quotes to compare. In areas without a station, such as Bopal or Shela, a nearby tutor who keeps a steady slot often matters more than the rate.

Can one tutor teach two siblings to save money?

Sometimes. It works most smoothly when the children are close in class and board, and the tutor teaches them one after the other in the same visit. Each tutor sets their own fee for this, so ask how two back-to-back sessions are priced and compare the total across your two or three matched tutors.
