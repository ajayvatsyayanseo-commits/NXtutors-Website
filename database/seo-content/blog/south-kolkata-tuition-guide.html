South Kolkata is where the city's metro story began, and it still shapes how a home tutor reaches a family. The Blue Line runs down the middle from Bhowanipore to Garia, the Purple Line follows Diamond Harbour Road through Behala, the Orange Line runs along the EM Bypass, and the Sealdah South suburban lines cut across all of it. A tutor who can ride one of these and finish by auto or rickshaw has far more homes within reach on a weekday evening.

This guide covers four zones: Ballygunge, Gariahat & Alipore; Tollygunge, Jadavpur & Garia; Behala & New Alipore; and Kasba & EM Bypass South. For each it shows which stations tutors use, what kind of homes they visit, whether the first visit starts at a door or a gate, and when to time a class.

How do the four zones fit together?

The Blue Line ties the first two zones together, the Purple Line serves the south-west and the Orange Line the south-east, so your nearest line is the quickest guide to which tutors can come.

Ballygunge, Gariahat and Alipore

This is the oldest part of the metro map. The first stretch of the Blue Line, from Esplanade to Netaji Bhavan, opened on 24 October 1984, and the section on to Tollygunge, with Jatin Das Park, Kalighat and Rabindra Sarobar, followed on 29 April 1986. So most homes in Bhowanipore and Kalighat are a metro ride and a short walk from a tutor coming from Tollygunge, Garia or north Kolkata.

Ballygunge mixes the large family houses built in the 1930s and 1940s with apartment buildings, and Gariahat Market spreads along Gariahat Road and the lanes around the crossing. Ballygunge Junction on the Sealdah South lines sits on its eastern side. The crossing is crowded on weekend evenings and in the festive season, so plan a weekday slot. Just south, Dhakuria grew as a denser area of family houses and small buildings, with its own station on the same suburban line.

Jodhpur Park is planned: in 1947 a co-operative housing society divided the land into about 450 residential plots, so the streets run in a regular pattern and most homes are independent houses where the tutor goes straight to the door. Lake Gardens, south of the Rabindra Sarobar lake, has both Rabindra Sarobar metro station and its own stop on the Budge Budge branch. Alipore, with old bungalows, government residences and newer apartment buildings, is served by Majerhat and Kidderpore on the Circular section; many larger homes have a staffed gate, so share the tutor's name in advance.

Tollygunge, Jadavpur and Garia

The Blue Line continues south through this zone. The extension from Tollygunge to Kavi Nazrul in Garia, with Netaji, Masterda Surya Sen and Gitanjali stations, opened on 22 August 2009, and Shahid Khudiram followed in October 2010. Tollygunge, the centre of the Bengali film industry, also has a station on the Budge Budge suburban line. Its older lanes mean doorstep visits, while buildings on the main roads usually have a guard who notes a visitor's name.

Much of the zone took its present shape after 1947. Refugees from East Pakistan set up colonies in Jadavpur, around forty of them by 1949, and families from East Bengal turned Naktala from farmland into a busy neighbourhood. Bansdroni, beside Masterda Surya Sen station, is almost wholly residential. Across these localities most homes are family houses and mid-sized apartment buildings. Jadavpur's 8B crossing is very busy at rush hours, so weekend or early-afternoon slots are easier there.

Golf Green is low-rise apartment buildings among green spaces, where a caretaker or the family usually lets the tutor in. Regent Park is mostly compact family flats, reached from Mahanayak Uttam Kumar, Netaji or Masterda Surya Sen. Baghajatin has its own station on the Sealdah South lines and sits close to the EM Bypass. Garia, at the southern edge on the Adi Ganga, has Kavi Nazrul and Shahid Khudiram on the Blue Line and Garia and New Garia on the suburban line. Kavi Subhash, the end of the Orange Line, is also close. The main road between Tollygunge and Garia is slow in the evening peak, so an afternoon or weekend-morning slot is easier to keep.

Behala and New Alipore

Behala is one of the oldest and largest residential parts of the city, spread across wards 115 to 132 and taking in Barisha, Sakherbazar, Parnasree Pally, Sarsuna and Joka, many of them once separate villages. Diamond Harbour Road is the main artery, and since 30 December 2022 the Purple Line has run along it from Joka to Taratala, with Behala Chowrasta station built directly over the Chowrasta crossing. The line reached Majerhat on 6 March 2024.

That makes the zone far easier for tutors than it used to be. From Thakurpukur station or any other stop, most homes are a short auto or rickshaw ride away. New Alipore was laid out in the 1950s as a planned suburb and is still organised in lettered blocks, with New Alipore and Majerhat stations on the Budge Budge section; give the tutor your block letter and house number.

Housing across the zone is dense and mixed: old family houses beside newer apartment buildings and builder floors. Houses mean a doorstep visit, while newer buildings may ask for a name at the gate. Diamond Harbour Road is slow in the evening peak, so tutors coming by road do better with an afternoon or weekend-morning slot, while metro riders can manage later.

Kasba and EM Bypass South

The EM Bypass, open since 1982, runs from Ultadanga in the north to Baruipur, and its southern stretch now carries the Orange Line. The section from Kavi Subhash to Hemanta Mukhopadhyay opened on 6 March 2024, with Satyajit Ray at Panchasayar, Jyotirindra Nandi at Mukundapur and Kavi Sukanta at Kalikapur, and the line reached Beleghata in August 2025, passing VIP Bazar on the way.

Kasba grew after a rail overbridge opened in 1978 and a connector road linked it to the bypass; older buildings fill the inner paras and newer apartment complexes stand near the bypass. Santoshpur, once marshy land, has family houses and apartment buildings and a local lake. Mukundapur is mostly apartments in newer residential projects, and Patuli is a planned township, designed for about 4,500 serviced plots, with blocks on both sides of the bypass connector.

Most complexes here register visitors at the gate, so send the tutor's name and flat number before the demo. Plot houses in Patuli and family houses in Santoshpur are doorstep visits. The bypass and its junctions are busy in the evening peak, so weekday afternoons or weekend mornings suit tutors coming by road.

When should a weekday class start?

  • Near a metro station: a tutor who rides the Blue, Purple or Orange Line can usually keep an early-evening slot, even when the roads above are slow.
  • On a main road: for homes reached along Diamond Harbour Road, Gariahat Road, the Tollygunge–Garia road or the EM Bypass, a slot soon after school or after the evening rush starts on time more often.
  • Behind a gate: share the tutor's name, your tower or block and the flat number before the first class, so the demo starts on time.
  • For a specialist subject: if no nearby tutor covers it, one online class a week alongside a home tutor saves a long cross-city trip.

Frequently asked questions

Which metro line is most useful for home tutors in South Kolkata?

The Blue Line, because it runs through the middle of the area from Rabindra Sadan and Kalighat to Tollygunge, Masterda Surya Sen and Kavi Nazrul. Behala depends on the Purple Line along Diamond Harbour Road, and the EM Bypass side, from Kasba to Mukundapur, on the Orange Line.

Do tutors need a gate pass in South Kolkata?

It depends on the home. Independent houses in Jodhpur Park, Dhakuria, Bansdroni or the older lanes of Tollygunge are doorstep visits. Apartment complexes near the EM Bypass, in Mukundapur, Kasba and parts of Alipore, usually register visitors, so give the tutor's name to the gate before the free demo class.

When is the easiest time for a home class in South Kolkata?

Weekday afternoons and weekend mornings suit tutors who travel by road, because Diamond Harbour Road, the Gariahat crossing and the EM Bypass are busy in the evening peak. A tutor who comes by metro and finishes by auto can usually keep an early-evening slot as well.

Is Behala easy for tutors to reach now?

Much easier than before. The Purple Line has run along Diamond Harbour Road from Joka to Taratala since December 2022 and on to Majerhat since March 2024, so most Behala and Thakurpukur homes are a short auto ride from a station. Share your pocket name and nearest stop with the tutor.
