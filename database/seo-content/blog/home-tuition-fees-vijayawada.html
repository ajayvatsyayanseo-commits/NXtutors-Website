Knowing the band is the easy part. The harder part is planning a whole year around it, because the hours a child needs change sharply between Class 10 and the Intermediate years. This guide sets out typical weekly hours stage by stage, shows how to compare quotes written in different ways, lists what to agree in writing, and marks the local weeks worth planning around.

How do the hours change from Class 6 to the Intermediate years?

Most of what a family spends follows the number of hours, not small differences between tutors' rates. Hours tend to climb in two steps: once in Class 9, when the Class 10 syllabus starts to take shape, and again in the two Intermediate years, when subjects deepen and many students add entrance preparation. Why treat the Intermediate years as one budget

Intermediate is a two-year course, and the second year builds on the first. Budgeting only for the second year often means first-year gaps eat the hours meant for revision. Two habits help:

  • Price both years together. Set a steady weekly load for the first year, then add hours in the second.
  • Keep entrance work in its own line. If your child is preparing for JEE or NEET alongside the course, note those hours separately.

Does the board change what a tutor in Vijayawada charges?

Rarely on its own. Children in the city study under the Board of Secondary Education, Andhra Pradesh (BSEAP) up to Class 10, and many continue with the Board of Intermediate Education, AP (BIEAP) for the two Intermediate years. Others study under CBSE or the CISCE's ICSE and ISC, and a smaller number need IB or IGCSE support. The board matters more for who you shortlist than for what you pay:

  • State board: ask which BSEAP or Intermediate classes the tutor has taught recently, and whether they explain in English, Telugu or a mix that suits your child's medium of study. The AP board tutor page for Vijayawada explains how to request one.
  • Moving from a state-board Class 10 to CBSE for Classes 11–12, or the other way: budget a few extra hours at the start of the new course to cover the change in textbooks and style of questions.
  • CBSE, ICSE and ISC: ask how recently the tutor taught your child's class under that board; see the CBSE and ICSE pages.
  • IB and IGCSE: expect quotes toward the upper end, and more for IB HL. An online tutor is often the practical route when no specialist lives within reach.

Hourly, per class or monthly: how do we compare quotes?

Your shortlist shows each tutor's fee before the demo, but the same money is sometimes described in different ways once lessons begin. Bring every quote back to an hourly figure.

How the fee is describedWhat to askHow to read it
Per hourIs a longer class charged pro rata?Already comparable; multiply by the weekly hours
Per classHow long is each class?Divide the class fee by its length in hours
Per monthHow many classes, of what length, in a month?Divide the monthly figure by the total hours
For a block or a termHow many hours, and what happens to a missed class?Divide by the hours, and agree the make-up rule in writing

What to agree in writing

  • The unit and the length. Whether the fee is per hour, per class or per month, and how long each class runs.
  • Missed classes. How much notice each side gives, and when a lost class is made up.
  • Extra classes before exams. Whether they are charged at the usual rate.
  • A review point. The next unit test, when you decide whether to keep, trim or stop the hours.

Does your part of Vijayawada change the cost?

Not the band. The locality changes how many tutors can reach your home at a fixed weekly time. Vijayawada has no metro yet; a network is planned but nothing is running, so tutors come by two-wheeler, auto, city bus or, from the outer side, by train to one of the satellite stations.

Which weeks of the Vijayawada year need room in the plan?

  • The months before the public exams. Class 10 and the Intermediate years end with board exams, and most families add hours in the run-up. Agree in advance whether extra classes are charged at the usual hourly rate.
  • Navaratri near the hill temple. Roads around the Kanaka Durga temple above the Krishna, close to Vidyadharapuram and the old city, become very crowded during the festival season. Moving a few classes online keeps the plan on track without changing the fee.
  • The Gunadala festival in February. The annual festival at the shrine on Gunadala hill draws very large crowds, so families near Gunadala and Eluru Road may reschedule or switch to online classes for those days.

One tutor for two subjects, or two tutors?

In the middle classes, one tutor for both maths and science keeps the timetable simple. From the Intermediate years, subjects usually need separate specialists. Ask each matched tutor which subjects they teach at your child's level, and compare the two plans using the hourly figures above.
