The rest of the bill comes from five choices a family makes: which subjects need a tutor, how many hours a week, where the child's board and class sit in the band, whether each subject is taught at home or online, and which tutors can reach your part of the city easily. This guide takes those five decisions in turn, then works through one example and lists what to settle before the first paid class.

Decision 1: which subjects really need a tutor?

Start with the subject that is actually holding the marks back, not every subject on the timetable:

  • Pick the subject with the widest gap between class work and test results, usually maths or a science in the senior classes.
  • Add a second subject only if the first plan is working and the second gap is clear from a recent test.
  • In Classes 11–12, treat physics, chemistry and maths separately, because one tutor rarely covers all three at the depth the board year needs.

Decision 4: at home, online or a mix?

Tutors set their own fees in both formats, so the choice is mostly about fit and travel rather than chasing a lower rate. A sensible split in many Kolkata homes:

  • At home: the subject that needs the most hands-on help, especially for younger children and for maths or science where working on paper together matters.
  • Online: a second subject, a specialist topic, or a board your nearest tutors do not teach. Online also removes the trip across the river or the bypass for the tutor.
  • A mix: one home visit plus one online class a week often keeps the hours up while cutting the number of trips.

What should we agree before the first paid class?

  • Payment timing: monthly in advance, or after the classes are held.
  • Missed classes: what happens when either side cancels, and how much notice is needed.
  • Festival weeks: whether classes pause over Durga Puja and other holidays, and how those weeks are counted.
  • Exam months: whether extra hours before tests and boards are at the same rate.
  • The review point: a date, such as the next unit test, when you check whether the plan is working.

Before any of this, the first class is a free demo, and switching tutor later is free, so a plan that is not working can be changed without starting the search again. Frequently asked questions

How much does a home tutor cost in Kolkata?

Across NXTutors, most home-tuition sessions fall between ₹800 and ₹2,500 an hour; Classes 11–12, IB/IGCSE and JEE/NEET sit toward the upper end; specialists for IB HL or JEE Advanced can charge more. Each tutor sets their own fee, which you see before the free demo class.

How do we estimate a monthly tuition bill?

Multiply the classes a week by their length to get weekly hours, then by four for a month, and price those hours at your tutor's quoted rate. Two one-hour classes a week make eight hours a month: ₹6,400 at the lower end of the band and ₹20,000 at the upper end.

Do WBBSE or WBCHSE students pay less than CBSE or ICSE students?

Not as a rule. Tutors for every board work across the same band, and each sets their own fee. The class and the depth needed matter more than the board: a Class 12 science student usually sits toward the upper end whichever board they follow, while junior classes sit lower.

Is home tuition dearer in Salt Lake or New Town than in North Kolkata?

The band is the same across the city. Where you live mainly decides how many tutors can reach you easily on a weekday, which changes how many quotes you can compare. A home near a metro or suburban station usually draws a wider shortlist than one reached only by road.

Can mixing home and online classes lower the monthly total?

It can, mainly by cutting hours and trips rather than rates. Keep one home tutor for the subject that needs hands-on help and take a second subject online, or drop to fewer longer visits. If the mix does not suit your child, switching tutor later is free on NXTutors.
