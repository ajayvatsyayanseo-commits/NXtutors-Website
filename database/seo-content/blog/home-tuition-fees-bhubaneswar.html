Why do visit length and frequency matter more than the rate?

What a family spends follows three things: the hourly fee, how long each visit lasts and how many visits happen in a week. Two families paying the same tutor the same rate can end up with very different bills because one books two one-hour visits and the other books four hour-and-a-half visits. You compare the rate between tutors; the length and frequency are yours to control, so a family that likes a tutor quoting toward the upper end can still shape a workable plan.

Is a longer visit always better value?

Not always. Concentration fades, and a tired child in the second hour learns less than a fresh one in the first. For younger children, two hours in one sitting is rarely the right answer, even when it saves the tutor a journey.

How does Odisha's two-board path affect the plan?

Families on the state board in Bhubaneswar deal with two separate bodies. The Board of Secondary Education, Odisha (BSE Odisha) conducts the Class 10 examination, and the Council of Higher Secondary Education, Odisha (CHSE) is responsible for Classes 11 and 12. Many other families study under CBSE or CISCE (ICSE and ISC), and some need IB or IGCSE help.

The board name rarely moves a fee on its own; class, subject and the tutor's experience matter more. What the board changes is the experience you look for, and the moment you may need to rethink the plan:

  • BSE Odisha, Class 10: ask which state-board classes the tutor has taught recently and whether they teach in Odia, English or both, so the lessons match your child's medium.
  • Moving up to CHSE: the step into Class 11 changes textbooks and depth. A Class 10 tutor who covered all subjects may not be the right person for higher secondary physics or chemistry, so review the pattern at this point rather than carrying it over unchanged.
  • CBSE: find out which CBSE classes the tutor taught last session, and whether practice uses the board's own sample question papers.
  • ICSE and ISC: check that the tutor knows the CISCE syllabus for your child's class from recent teaching.
  • IB and IGCSE: expect quotes toward the upper end; an IB HL specialist can charge more.

For exact syllabus or exam rules, check the board's own official website.

Yes, mainly by removing travel from the decision. Which details should we agree about the visit itself?

  1. Length in writing. State whether a visit is one hour, an hour and a half, or two hours, and that it starts when the lesson starts, not when the tutor reaches the gate.
  2. Running over. Agree whether a visit that overruns is charged, or treated as part of the plan.
  3. Cancellations. Settle how much notice either side gives and how a missed visit is made up.
  4. Exam weeks. Ask whether extra visits before tests and board examinations are charged at the usual rate.
  5. Payment timing. Decide whether fees are paid at the start of the month or after the visits are held.
  6. A review point. Pick a date, such as the next unit test, to ask whether the length and frequency are still right.
