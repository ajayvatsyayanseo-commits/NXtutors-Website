Jammu sits on both banks of the Tawi. The old city stands on the right bank and looks over the river from the north, while the planned colonies of the new city, from Gandhi Nagar to the Trikuta Nagar sectors and the Channi colonies, spread across the left bank. Jammu Tawi railway station is on the new city side. Which bank you live on, and which roads lead to your door, shape how a tutor reaches you and which weekly slot is realistic.

Below, each of the five NXTutors zones in Jammu is taken in turn, with a note on every locality.

How do home tutors travel around Jammu?

By road, mostly on the tutor's own two-wheeler, sometimes by car, auto or mini-bus. The elevated road from Bikram Chowk carries through traffic into Gandhi Nagar, the road via Jewel Chowk joins the old city with Gandhi Nagar, bridges over the Tawi link the two banks, and the NH-44 bypass from Kunjwani runs along the eastern edge to Sidhra.

Two road projects affect timing for now: a flyover from Satwari Chowk to Last Morh in Gandhi Nagar is under construction, and a four-lane flyover from Kunjwani to Satwari is being completed. Expect diversions there.

Rail Head & New City

  • Gandhi Nagar: a main planned colony with a large market, private houses, newer flats and single-storey government housing. Gol Market Chowk and Last Morh are the usual landmarks. Market roads fill in the evening, so fix a slot with margin.
  • Nanak Nagar: an established colony large enough to form three municipal wards, mostly independent houses with some builder floors. It suits a tutor who already teaches in Gandhi Nagar or Trikuta Nagar. Share a lane landmark before the first visit.
  • Shastri Nagar: a Housing Board colony and a ward of its own, with two-bedroom homes the most common. A loop road links it with Gandhi Nagar and the national highway. Buildings with several flats may want the tutor's name at the entrance.

Trikuta & Channi

  • Trikuta Nagar: a development authority colony laid out from Sector 1 to Sector 9, including 5A, with its own shopping areas. A sector and house number usually find the door; junctions near the station and bypass are busy at peak hours.
  • Channi Himmat: a Housing Board colony of numbered sectors and independent houses. A road through Sectors 4 and 7 to the bypass at Deeli helps tutors from the highway side.
  • Channi Rama: a residential area next to Channi Himmat, still seeing new construction. Newer pockets can be hard to find, so send a map pin, and tell the guard about the tutor if you live in a flat.
  • Bathindi: also written Bhatindi, a fast-growing area of new flats and houses. Bhatindi Morh, where it meets NH-44, is the landmark. Traffic builds at that junction in the evening, so choose a class time with a cushion.

Kunjwani & Sainik Colony

Colonies along NH-44, with Bari Brahmana and Jammu Tawi as the nearest stations.

  • Sainik Colony: a large colony split into two wards, mostly independent houses, with plots still being built on in the Extension. A tutor living in the colony or in Greater Kailash keeps weekday classes steady.
  • Greater Kailash: a ward of its own with houses and apartment buildings. Trikuta Nagar Chowk, also called Greater Kailash Chowk, is the landmark; avoid the evening peak there.
  • Kunjwani: a busy junction locality where the Pathankot and Bishnah roads meet the city and the bypass begins. Homes sit in lanes off the highway, so a lane landmark avoids confusion near the chowk.

Old City & Sidhra

  • Old City: Raghunath Bazar, Purani Mandi, Pacca Danga, Kanak Mandi and nearby neighbourhoods, where narrow lanes open into small squares. A two-wheeler and a precise lane landmark help, and some lanes are simpler on foot.
  • Rehari Colony: usually just Rehari, split into North and South wards, with Rehari Chowk and Rehari Chungi as landmarks. Tutors from the old city, Bakshi Nagar or Janipur reach it without crossing the river.
  • Bakshi Nagar: a long-settled area of independent houses between Rehari, Talab Tillo and the old city. The Bakshi Nagar road gives a direct route from Akhnoor Road, so tutors from the Talab Tillo side arrive easily.
  • Sidhra: a fast-developing area on the Tawi in the Shivalik foothills, with new houses and apartment buildings, and the proposed site of a satellite township. Tutors come over the bridge from the old city or along the bypass; complexes may register visitors at the gate.

Janipur & Akhnoor Road

  • Janipur: an established area of four wards with a development authority colony and New Janipur along the foothills. Lane parking is tight, so a tutor on a two-wheeler and an early-evening slot work well.
  • Talab Tillo: a residential and commercial area around Talab Tillo Chowk, taking in Udheywala, Anand Nagar, Gol Pulli and Pakka Gharat. The chowk gets crowded in the evening, so give a lane landmark and plan with margin.
  • Paloura: three Paloura wards on the north-west, much of the newer housing in plotted layouts around Paloura Chowk. Roads arrive from Rehari, Janipur and Akhnoor Road; send a map pin for a first visit.
  • Roop Nagar: a planned development authority colony of houses along the foothills, near New Janipur and Bantalab, with its own shopping area. The nearest tutors usually live in Roop Nagar, Janipur or Paloura.

Timing and boards across Jammu

In most localities the evening market hours, more than distance, decide whether a class starts on time. Pick a fixed weekday slot, allow for the flyover diversions, and keep an online back-up for slow days. Where a specialist lives on the far bank, an online class often beats a cross-city trip.

Children in Jammu study under the Jammu and Kashmir Board of School Education (JKBOSE), CBSE, or the CISCE's ICSE and ISC, and some families look for IB or IGCSE help. Frequently asked questions

Should we look for a tutor on our own side of the Tawi?

Usually, yes. The old city and the northern colonies sit on the right bank, while the new city, Trikuta Nagar, the Channi colonies and Sainik Colony are on the left. A tutor living on your bank finds a weekly slot easier to keep. NXTutors starts with nearby tutors, then widens to your zone and online.

How will the flyover work affect class timing?

Work on the Satwari–Last Morh flyover and the completion of the Kunjwani–Satwari flyover bring diversions around Gandhi Nagar, Satwari and Kunjwani for now. Families nearby should choose a slot with some margin, avoid the evening peak, and agree that a class can move online on a day the roads are badly delayed.

What should we tell a tutor before the first visit in Trikuta Nagar?

Give the sector number and house number, which are usually enough to find a home in the colony's planned layout, along with a phone number. Arrival is at the doorstep. If you live near the station or bypass junctions, pick a weekday time outside the busiest hours so the class starts on schedule.

Is it hard for a tutor to reach homes in the old city?

Not if you plan for the lanes. Raghunath Bazar, Kanak Mandi Road and Residency Road are among the busiest streets, and some lanes are easier on foot. Share a precise lane landmark, expect the tutor to come by two-wheeler, and agree a fixed time away from the evening market rush.

Can families in Sidhra or Bathindi get a home tutor?

Yes, request one as usual. Both are growing areas with many new flats, so share the building name and tell the gate the tutor's name. NXTutors looks for tutors nearby first, then across your zone and the city, and suggests an online tutor where a specialist subject teacher lives far away.
