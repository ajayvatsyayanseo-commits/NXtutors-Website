Jamshedpur is a planned city shaped by two rivers. It grew from Sakchi, the village chosen in 1904 as the site for the steel plant around which the city was built, and it sits where the Kharkai meets the Subarnarekha. The Kharkai separates Adityapur and Gamharia from the city on the west; the Subarnarekha separates Mango and Dimna on the north. For a home tutor, which bank you live on often matters more than distance.

This guide covers all five NXTutors zones in Jamshedpur: Central Jamshedpur; West Jamshedpur & Kharkai Side; South Jamshedpur & Tatanagar; East Jamshedpur; and Mango & Dimna. Locality by locality, you will find the housing a tutor walks into, which river or road they cross to get there, and the hours to keep clear.

Which routes and stations serve each zone?

There is no metro in Jamshedpur. Tutors ride two-wheelers, take autos or book cabs. Tatanagar Junction, opened in 1910 and originally called Kalimati, is the main railway station, and smaller stations at Adityapur, Gamharia, Salgajhari and Govindpur help tutors who live further out.

Central Jamshedpur

The central zone holds the city's earliest settlements and busiest markets. Sakchi Market is the oldest market in the city, and Sakchi Golchakkar is one of its busiest crossings. Bistupur, one of the earliest settlements laid out during the planning of Jamshedpur, is now the main business address.

  • Bistupur: showrooms, hotels, restaurants and offices, with homes mostly in two- and three-bedroom apartments and some older bungalows in the quieter lanes. The Kharkai runs along its western side, with a bridge to Adityapur. Evening slots run more smoothly away from the market rush.
  • Sakchi: older houses in busy lanes and apartment buildings with two- and three-bedroom flats, bounded by the Subarnarekha to the north. Tutors avoid market hours. Buses and autos cross from here to Mango.
  • Circuit House Area: a compact residential pocket sharing the 831001 postcode with Sakchi and Bistupur, mostly independent houses with a few flats near the main road. Tutors from Sakchi, Bistupur, Kadma or Sonari reach it without crossing a river, which makes regular weekday slots easy.
  • Golmuri: a residential area known for Golmuri Market, with houses and flats and quiet, green streets away from the market. Tatanagar is the main station and the smaller Salgajhari halt is also used.
  • Sidhgora: a locality close to the steel works, next to Agrico, Bhalubasa, Golmuri and Baradwari. Housing is mostly independent houses and plots, so most visits are to a family home rather than a gated complex.

West Jamshedpur & Kharkai Side

The western zone spans both banks of the Kharkai. Adityapur is a separate municipal corporation in Seraikela Kharsawan district, and two bridges cross the Kharkai into the city, one to Bistupur and one to Kadma. Sonari's northern edge reaches Domuhani, the meeting point of the two rivers, and Marine Drive links Sonari with Kadma, Adityapur, Sakchi and Bistupur.

  • Kadma: a large residential neighbourhood with older company quarters and privately built apartment buildings. Quarters are simple doorstep visits; apartment buildings may keep a visitor register. Book around the evening rush near Kadma Market.
  • Sonari: divided into North, West, East and South layouts, with many housing societies and older houses. Tutors should expect gate registration and visitor parking rules in the societies. The Domuhani bridge over the Subarnarekha links Sonari with Dobo on the Chandil side.
  • Adityapur: surrounded on three sides by the Kharkai, with an industrial area beside residential colonies of houses, plots and apartment societies. Adityapur station is on the Howrah to Mumbai line. Avoid the bridges at peak hours.
  • Gamharia: on the western edge along the Kandra road, with its own industrial area and a growing residential side of plots and independent houses. Gamharia Junction serves local families. Tutors from Adityapur are the most practical for home visits.

South Jamshedpur & Tatanagar

The southern zone is built around Tatanagar Junction, which lies on the Howrah to Mumbai line, the Asansol to Kharagpur line and the branch towards Badampahar. Jugsalai, next to the station, has its own municipal council, and Parsudih lies on the far side of the station towards the Chaibasa highway.

  • Jugsalai: often called the wholesale market of Jamshedpur, with apartment buildings put up by local builders and older family homes in the market lanes. Market streets are crowded during trading hours, so tutors prefer early morning or later evening slots and park just off the main lanes.
  • Burmamines: also written Burma Mines, next to Parsudih on the far side of the station, with Jugsalai and Telco Colony close by. Homes are largely independent houses, so tutors arrive at the door and park outside.
  • Parsudih: parts include Haludbani and Khasmahal, with private houses and apartment buildings rather than large gated complexes. The Chaibasa road is the main approach, and school-time traffic near the station crossing is the thing to plan around.

East Jamshedpur

The eastern zone is a spread of planned colonies and plotted neighbourhoods, including Telco Colony, Birsanagar, Baridih and Govindpur. Birsanagar alone is divided into twelve zones, and Straight Mile Road connects Baridih with the centre.

  • Telco Colony: a planned township built to house the workforce of a nearby vehicle works, with township quarters and privately built apartment buildings. Quarters are easy doorstep visits; apartment buildings may ask tutors to sign in. Salgajhari is the nearest station, and slots are easiest once factory shift traffic clears.
  • Birsanagar: mostly independent houses on plotted lanes, spread across twelve zones. Share the zone number and a landmark such as Birsanagar Market or Plaza Market when booking a demo.
  • Baridih: apartments and houses at one end of Straight Mile Road, near Birsanagar and Vijaya Gardens, with Baridih Market as the local shopping point. Weekday evening slots are simplest once office traffic on Straight Mile Road eases.
  • Govindpur: known for affordable plots and independent houses, near Gadhra and Jojobera, with its own passenger halt on the Howrah to Mumbai line. Most visits are doorstep arrivals with space to park.

Mango & Dimna

Across the Subarnarekha, Mango has its own civic body and has grown from a small town into a large residential suburb. It is linked to Sakchi by three bridges built side by side, and NH 18 runs through the area on the Dimna to Pardih stretch. An elevated corridor is being built on this stretch, from Pardih to Baliguma via Dimna Chowk.

  • Mango: apartment complexes, builder buildings and independent houses near Jharkhand Colony and Pardih. The Mango bridge and the Pardih and Dimna junctions get heavy traffic, so tutors plan visits outside the busiest hours, and a tutor living in Mango is often the easiest choice.
  • Dimna: apartment buildings and independent houses near Mango, Jharkhand Colony and Ripit Colony, with easy auto and cab availability. Traffic at Dimna Chowk can be heavy, so choose off-peak slots and share a landmark near the chowk.

Boards and board years in Jamshedpur

Schools across Jamshedpur follow the Jharkhand Academic Council (JAC), the state's own board, or CBSE, or the CISCE's ICSE and ISC, and a few families need IB or IGCSE help. Put the board in your request; with JAC, add the language your child writes the exam in so the shortlist matches it. Board-year students in Class 10 and Class 12 gain most from a tutor booked at the start of the session, not after the half-yearly exams.

Frequently asked questions

With no metro, how do tutors travel across Jamshedpur?

Mostly on two-wheelers, with autos and cabs as back-up, and buses and autos run regularly between Sakchi and Mango. Some tutors living further out ride a train to Tatanagar, Adityapur or Gamharia, then take an auto. Picking someone from your own bank of the river keeps travel predictable.

Does living in Adityapur or Mango limit the choice of tutors?

Not much, but it changes the order. NXTutors lists tutors on your own side of the river first, then the rest of the zone and city, then online tutors. A tutor who does not need to cross a bridge at rush hour is more likely to keep a steady weekly slot.

Which parts of Jamshedpur have mostly independent houses?

Birsanagar, Govindpur, Burmamines and Sidhgora are largely independent houses and plots, and Circuit House Area is mostly houses too. Tutor visits there are usually doorstep arrivals, often with parking right outside. Apartment societies in Sonari, Adityapur and Mango normally ask visitors to register at the gate.

How should classes be timed near Tatanagar?

Away from train times and school hours. The station roads are the main way into Burmamines and Parsudih, and traffic near the station crossing builds at those times. In Jugsalai, avoid trading hours on the market streets; early morning or later evening slots are usually easier to keep.
