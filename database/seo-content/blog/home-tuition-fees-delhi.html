Why budget by the goal rather than the month?

A monthly figure hides the question most parents need answered first: how long will this go on? Help with one weak chapter is short. Support through a board year runs from the April start of the session to the exams. When you price the goal as a whole, weekly hours times the number of weeks gives the total hours, and the total hours at both ends of the band give the full commitment before the first paid class.

How do we turn a goal into a plan we can afford?

  1. Write the goal in one line. "Class 9 maths: catch up on algebra before the half-yearly exam" is a goal. "Extra help" is not.
  2. Set an end point. Use a date, such as the half-yearly or the pre-boards, or a result you can check, such as the next unit test.
  3. Price the total hours at both ends of the band. If the upper figure is out of reach, cut the weekly hours or shorten the goal before you start, not halfway through.
  4. Decide in advance what happens at the end point. Stop, drop to one class a week, or carry on with a new goal.
  5. Review at the halfway mark. If the gap is closing faster than planned, reduce the hours. If it is not, talk to the tutor about the plan before adding more hours.

When is a quote toward the upper end worth it?

The higher part of the band usually earns its place when:

  • the student is in Class 11 or 12 and needs a separate tutor for each senior science subject;
  • the programme has its own assessment style, as IB and IGCSE do, and the tutor needs to know it well;
  • the goal is JEE, NEET or IB HL, where specialist depth matters more than the hourly rate;
  • the student has changed board mid-way and needs a structured catch-up;
  • earlier tutors have not moved the results, and experience with your exact board and class is the missing piece.

The lower part of the band usually covers junior classes, homework support and steady practice in a subject the child already follows, where regular attendance matters more than specialist depth.

How does your part of Delhi shape the plan?

Location rarely sets a fee on its own; it decides how many tutors can reach you easily and how reliably a class starts on time. In Delhi the metro does much of that work, and a home within reach of a station can draw tutors from anywhere along the line, which means more quotes to compare.

How much does a home tutor cost in Delhi?

How do we work out the total cost of a tuition goal?

Multiply the weekly hours by the number of weeks to get the total hours, then price those hours at ₹800 and at ₹2,500. Three hours a week for eight weeks is twenty-four hours: ₹19,200 at the lower end of the band and ₹60,000 at the upper end.

Is home tuition dearer in South Delhi than in East or West Delhi?

The band is the same across Delhi. Class, board, subject and the tutor's experience set most of a fee, and each tutor sets their own. Location mainly decides how many tutors can reach you easily, which changes how many quotes you can compare rather than the band itself.

Does living near a metro station lower the fee?

Not directly. A home near a station can be reached by tutors who travel by metro from further along the line, so your shortlist can draw from a wider area and you may have more quotes to compare. A fixed weekly slot, set away from the office rush, helps any class start on time.

Can mixing home and online classes stretch a Delhi budget?

Often, yes. Keep a home tutor for the subject that needs the most hands-on help and take a second subject, or a specialist board, online. That cuts the number of trips each week and widens the choice of tutors. Switching tutor later is free on NXTutors if the mix does not suit.
