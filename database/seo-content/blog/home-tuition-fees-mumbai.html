What moves a quote, and what only changes the plan?

It helps to keep two lists apart.

  • Things that move the rate: the class, the board, the subject, an entrance goal such as JEE or NEET, and the tutor's depth in that exact syllabus. An IB HL tutor and a Class 6 homework tutor are pricing different work.
  • Things that change the plan, not the band: where you live, how the tutor travels, and whether classes are at home or online. These decide how many tutors can reach you on a weekday evening and how many quotes you get to compare.

Paying more for a tutor who is further away rarely makes sense when a tutor with the same board depth lives on your own rail line. Paying more for depth, when the goal is a senior class or a specialist programme, often does.

How does the rail line near you shape the shortlist?

In Mumbai, the line a tutor lives on matters more than the distance on a map. A home a short auto ride from a suburban station or a metro stop can draw tutors from anywhere along that line.

Along the Western line

From Churchgate through Dadar, Bandra, Andheri and Borivali to Dahisar, the Western line and the Harbour line's western branch carry most tutor trips on the west side. Metro Lines 1, 2A and 7 fill the gaps between the stations. See the western suburbs guide and the South and Central Mumbai guide.

Along the Central line

Dadar, Sion, Ghatkopar, Kanjurmarg, Bhandup and Mulund lie on the Central line, with the Harbour line serving Chembur and Metro Line 1 linking Ghatkopar with Andheri. Metro Line 4 is under construction along this side and does not yet carry passengers. See the central suburbs guide.

Thane and across the creek

Thane is on the Central line and is where the Trans-Harbour line begins. That line runs through Airoli, Ghansoli, Kopar Khairane and Vashi towards Panvel, while the Harbour line serves Vashi, Nerul, Belapur and Panvel from the Mumbai side. Parts of Thane's Ghodbunder Road belt have no station yet, so tutors there come by road. See the Thane and Navi Mumbai guide.

How should the budget handle the monsoon and exam season?

Three moments in the Mumbai school year deserve a line in the budget before they arrive.

  1. Heavy-rain days. Agree in advance that a class moves online if travel is disrupted, at the same rate and time. That keeps the weekly hours intact without a missed-class argument.
  2. Before school and board exams. Many families want extra hours in the last stretch. Ask now whether those hours are at the same rate, and cap them: an extra hour a week for a set number of weeks is easier to plan than an open-ended increase.
  3. After results. Decide whether tuition pauses, drops to one class a week, or starts on next year's syllabus. A short pause costs nothing and gives the student a clean start.

Which money questions should we settle before the first paid class?

  • Is payment monthly in advance, or after the classes are held?
  • What happens when either side cancels, and how much notice is needed?
  • If a class is cut short, is the time made up or adjusted?
  • Does the rate change for extra hours before exams?
  • If two children in the family learn together, how is that priced?
  • If the tutor also offers online sessions, is the rate the same?

Write the answers in a message to the tutor, so both sides have the same record.

Is tuition dearer in South Mumbai than in the suburbs?

The band is the same across the city. The class, board, subject and the tutor's depth in that syllabus set most of a fee. Where you live mainly decides how many tutors can reach you on a weekday evening, which changes how many quotes you can compare.

What happens to a class on a heavy-rain day?

Settle it before the monsoon. Many families agree that the class moves online at the usual time and rate when travel is disrupted, so the week's hours are not lost. Put the arrangement in a message to the tutor so both sides remember it later.

Can we mix home and online classes to manage cost?

Yes. Keep a home tutor for the subject that needs the most hands-on help and take a second subject, or a specialist programme such as IB or IGCSE, online. That cuts weekly trips and widens the choice of tutors. Switching tutor later is free if the mix does not suit.
