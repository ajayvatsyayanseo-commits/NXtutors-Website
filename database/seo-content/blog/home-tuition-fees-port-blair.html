The medium of instruction is part of the match

This is the question families in the islands most often forget to mention. The South Andaman district administration's education page records schooling in five mediums: English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu and Bengali. The government school list includes a Tamil-medium primary school on the Aberdeen side and Telugu-medium schools at Haddo, all within the CBSE system at secondary level.

A child in a Hindi- or Telugu-medium class may follow the same syllabus as an English-medium classmate yet need a tutor who can explain a hard chapter in the home language. State the medium in your request alongside the class and subjects. It rarely moves a rate on its own, but it narrows the list to the tutors who can actually help, and it saves a wasted demo.

CBSE in the islands: why it simplifies the search

The South Andaman district administration states that all secondary and senior secondary schools are affiliated to CBSE, and the Directorate of Education's school portal lists government secondary or senior secondary schools at Aberdeen, Haddo, School Line, Junglighat, Delanipur, Dairy Farm, South Point and Prothrapur. No separate island school board is named on these official pages.

For the fee question, a single board has one useful effect: almost every local tutor already works from the same textbooks and exam pattern, so you are comparing teaching skill, not syllabus knowledge. Ship days, the monsoon and the school calendar

An hourly rate means little if half the classes do not happen. Three things in the islands can break a weekly pattern, and each is easier to plan for at the start than in the middle of term.

  • Ship days. Families who travel between islands for work, or whose children go with them, miss a run of classes. Agree in advance whether those weeks move online.
  • Heavy rain. In the monsoon months an evening home visit can be harder to keep. Decide what happens on a very wet day: a later start, an online class, or a make-up visit at the weekend.
  • Tests and board papers. Senior students often want extra classes before school tests and the CBSE board papers. Settle how many, for which chapters, and when the extra hours stop.

Questions to put to each tutor before you choose

Ask every tutor the same short set of questions and write the answers side by side.

  1. Is your rate per hour, per class or per month, and how long is one class by the clock?
  2. How many classes a week do you suggest for this class and subject, and why?
  3. Can you explain in the medium my child studies in, if needed?
  4. Do you set homework between visits, and when do you check it?
  5. Does an online class carry the same rate as a home visit?
  6. How much notice do you need to cancel, and how is a missed class made up?
  7. When shall we review progress together, for example after the first unit test?

A tutor who answers these clearly before the first paid class is usually the one who stays organised through the year.
